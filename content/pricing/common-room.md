## A restructure that doubled the floor

Common Room's 2026 pricing change is the story here. The company eliminated both its Starter tier, which entered at $1,000 a month, and its Team tier at $30,000 a year. What remains is three plans — Essential, Advanced and Enterprise — with only Essential carrying a published price of $2,100 a month, billed annually.

For anyone who evaluated Common Room previously, that is a 110% increase in the cost of entry. And because the old tiers are extensively documented across comparison articles and cached blog posts, a large amount of the pricing information you will encounter describes plans that cannot be purchased.

## What the new floor implies about the buyer

Pricing changes are positioning changes. A $12,000 annual minimum admits a small startup team testing whether community and signal data helps them. A $25,200 minimum — explicitly before add-ons — does not.

The practical reading is that Common Room has moved from a tool a growth-minded team could adopt on a modest budget to a platform that assumes a funded go-to-market function with a defined signal strategy. If you are evaluating it as a two-person startup, the price is now telling you something the sales conversation may not.

## Justifying the number

Common Room's proposition is signal capture: surfacing intent that your CRM cannot see — community activity, job changes, product usage, social engagement, champion movement — and routing it to the right seller at the right time.

That value is genuinely attributable, which makes the business case unusually testable. At $25,200 a year, the question is whether the platform surfaces enough incremental pipeline to cover itself. For a company with a $60,000 average contract value and a 25% win rate, two additional closed deals a year clears the cost twice over. For a company selling $500 subscriptions, the arithmetic never works no matter how good the signals are.

Do that calculation with your own ACV before the demo, not after.

## The unpublished majority

Only one of three tiers has a public price. Advanced and Enterprise — the plans most mid-market and larger teams will actually be steered toward — are quoted.

That matters more than it usually would, because the published Essential rate anchors expectations low. Given that the retired Team tier was $30,000 a year, it is reasonable to expect Advanced to land above Essential's $25,200 by a meaningful margin. Ask for both quotes up front, even if you intend to buy Essential, so that the upgrade path is a known number rather than a surprise in year two.

## What to negotiate

Annual commitment with no published monthly option means you are betting $25,200 on signal quality you have not yet observed in your own market. The single most valuable thing to ask for is a signal assessment against your actual target accounts before you sign — Common Room can run its capture over a defined account list and show you what it finds.

If the answer is thin, that is the cheapest possible way to learn it. If it is rich, you have a stronger position on price than a cold negotiation would give you.

## Who should and should not buy this

Common Room fits venture-backed B2B software companies with community-led or product-led motions, meaningful contract values, and a go-to-market team equipped to act on signals rather than just collect them. Developer tools, open-source commercial companies and API businesses are the archetype — places where the buyer shows up in a Slack community or a GitHub repo long before they show up in your CRM.

It fits poorly for small teams after the 2026 restructure, which removed the tier they used to buy. It fits poorly for low-ACV businesses, where no amount of signal quality clears a $25,200 hurdle. And it fits poorly for sales-led enterprise motions with no community or product footprint, since the signals it is best at capturing simply do not exist for that model.

Common Room changed pricing significantly in 2026 and may do so again. Confirm current tiers directly at commonroom.io before relying on any published figure — including this one.
