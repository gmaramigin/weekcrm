## One price, nine modules, one catch

AntMyERP does something rare in this category: it refuses to build a tier ladder. There is no Starter plan missing reporting, no Professional plan that unlocks automation. Every customer pays $50 per user per month — ₹1,200 in India — and receives all nine modules, from CRM and Sales through Inventory, Finance, HR and Supply Chain.

That simplicity is real and worth something. Most ERP-adjacent vendors monetise confusion, gating the module you actually need behind the tier above the one you budgeted for. AntMyERP does not play that game.

The catch is on a different axis entirely. AntMyERP enforces a **ten-user minimum**. The pricing page leads with $50; the smallest invoice it will issue is $500 per month.

## What the floor does to small teams

| Team size | Seats billed | Monthly cost | Effective per-person rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 people | 10 | $500 | $125 |
| 7 people | 10 | $500 | $71 |
| 10 people | 10 | $500 | $50 |
| 25 people | 25 | $1,250 | $50 |

A six-person service company pays $83 per actual human per month — not $50. The advertised rate only becomes the real rate once headcount reaches ten, and everything below that is a surcharge for being small.

This matters because AntMyERP's natural buyer — a field-service or equipment-maintenance business — is frequently a six-to-twelve person operation. Half of that target market sits on the wrong side of the minimum.

## Where the rest of the money goes

Two costs never appear in the per-user figure.

**Implementation** is quoted separately, scoped by user count and business complexity. This is not padding: standing up nine interlocking modules with real inventory and finance data is a project, and AntMyERP assigns an implementation coordinator to run it. But it means the first-year number is the subscription plus an unquantified one-off, and you cannot budget the second without asking.

**Storage** is 20 GB pooled across the entire account rather than allocated per user. For a CRM that would be generous. For a service platform where technicians attach photographs to job tickets, it is a ceiling you will meet — and one that arrives independently of how many seats you bought.

Local taxes sit on top of everything, and subscriptions are explicitly non-refundable. That last term deserves attention: with a ten-seat floor and no refunds, the 15-day free trial is not a formality, it is the only point at which the decision is reversible. Load real data into it.

## The offer that changes the maths

AntMyERP has run a standing "10 users free forever" promotion. If it is live when you evaluate, the calculus inverts completely — a ten-person business runs the whole nine-module suite at zero, and only starts paying at the eleventh seat. That is an aggressive land-and-expand play, and for a small service company it is close to unbeatable.

Treat it as a promotion, not a plan. Confirm it directly with the vendor before you build a budget on it, and understand what the eleventh user costs when you cross that line.

## Who should buy this

AntMyERP fits service and equipment businesses with at least ten staff that are currently running a CRM, a spreadsheet inventory and an accounting package that do not speak to each other. At $50 all-in, replacing three tools with one is straightforward value, and the free unlimited customer and vendor portals remove a cost that other vendors charge for.

It is poor value below ten users, where the minimum inflates the effective rate by 25–150%. And it is a weak fit for teams that want only CRM — you are paying for Finance, HR and Supply Chain whether or not you switch them on, which is the flip side of a bundle with no tiers.

Pricing and the lifetime offer both change. Check antmyerp.com/pricing for current terms before committing.
