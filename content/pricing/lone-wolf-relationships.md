## A price range that spans a factor of ten

Lone Wolf does not publish rates. What is reported by third parties is a starting price of roughly $25 a month — though some sources say $33.25 — and a Professional plan at $249.

That is a tenfold spread across one product line, and it is not a sign of a complicated feature ladder. It is a sign that Lone Wolf Relationships is sold to two quite different buyers: individual agents who want lead generation and client management for the price of a streaming subscription, and teams or high-volume producers who need considerably more.

## What the reported tiers look like

| Configuration | Reported price | Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | ~$25–$33.25/mo | individual agent |
| Professional | $249/mo | teams, high-volume agents |
| Brokerage / franchise | custom | brokerages, franchise networks |

The disagreement between $25 and $33.25 at the entry level tells you these are not published numbers being reported accurately — they are quotes being relayed second-hand, probably from different configurations or promotional periods. Neither should be treated as your price.

## The Professional jump

Going from roughly $25 to $249 is not an upgrade in the usual sense. A tenfold price step almost always means a different product configuration or a different buyer segment rather than a set of additional features.

That makes it the most important thing to establish in a sales conversation. Ask directly what triggers the move to Professional, what specifically it includes, and whether there is anything between the two. If the honest answer is that Professional is the team product and entry is the solo product, then the decision is about which one you are rather than which features you want.

## Add-ons without prices

EZ Texting appears alongside the product without published pricing. Messaging costs in real estate CRM are rarely trivial — SMS is how agents actually reach leads — and an unquantified add-on attached to an unpublished base price makes the total genuinely unknowable from outside.

Ask for messaging as a separate line with a volume assumption. It is the add-on most likely to matter and the one least likely to be volunteered.

## Portfolio confusion

Lone Wolf has consolidated several real estate products through acquisition, including Propertybase. Those products overlap, and quotes occasionally get compared across them by mistake.

The two are not equivalent. Propertybase is a Salesforce-based residential CRM at $79 to $159 per user with a ten-user minimum and a substantial implementation. Relationships is the lighter agent-focused product starting around $25. If you are being quoted by Lone Wolf, confirm which product the number describes, and if you are being offered both, understand that they solve different problems at different scales.

## Approaching the quote

With no published pricing, three things give you leverage. Start the free trial before the sales conversation, so you are evaluating from experience rather than from a demo. Ask for the entry rate, the Professional rate and the trigger between them as three separate answers. And if you are a brokerage, ask what volume discount applies at your agent count and at the count you expect in two years, since brokerage pricing is explicitly negotiated.

## Who should and should not buy this

Lone Wolf Relationships fits individual agents and small teams who want lead generation and client management without a four-figure monthly platform commitment. At a reported $25 to $33 a month it sits at the accessible end of real estate CRM, and for an agent whose alternative is a spreadsheet that is a straightforward decision.

It fits poorly for buyers who need pricing transparency, since nothing is published and reported figures disagree. It fits poorly for teams sitting between the entry tier and Professional, where a tenfold step offers no middle ground. And it fits poorly for brokerages wanting a predictable published rate, since that segment is entirely custom-quoted.

All figures here are reported by third parties rather than published by Lone Wolf. Confirm current pricing, add-on costs and which product your quote covers at lwolf.com before budgeting.
