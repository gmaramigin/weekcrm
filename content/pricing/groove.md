## Three prices, one real variable

Groove's tiers look like a standard good-better-best ladder, and the feature lists reinforce that impression. But look at what changes between them and one number dominates: shared inboxes go from 2 to 5 to 25.

For most support teams, that is the entire buying decision. Two inboxes covers support@ and one other address. Five covers a company with separate billing, partner and regional queues. Twenty-five covers a multi-brand operation. Everything else — SLA management, skill-based routing, the AI features — is packaging that follows the inbox count rather than driving it.

The Lite User row is the sleeper. On Standard, every person who wants to read a customer thread is a full paid seat. On Plus, ten of them are free; on Pro, fifty. For a company where engineers and account managers routinely get looped into support conversations, that allowance is worth more than the $8 per-seat difference suggests — ten Lite Users on Plus would be $120 a month of Standard seats.

## The annual-only reality

Every advertised price requires annual billing. Groove does not publish a month-to-month equivalent at these rates, which means the real entry cost is a year's commitment: $720 for five agents on Standard, $1,200 on Plus.

That is normal for the segment but it changes how you should evaluate. There is no cheap way to run Groove alongside your existing tool for two months and compare. Use the trial period seriously, migrate a real inbox into it, and make the decision before you commit twelve months.

## AI: two different pricing models in one product

Groove splits its AI into two buckets and prices them differently. Summarisation, sentiment analysis, auto-tagging and writing assist are bundled into Plus and Pro at no extra charge. AI Drafts — where the system writes the reply — are metered at $0.25 per credit.

That split is defensible and it is also where the bill becomes unpredictable. A five-agent Plus team pays $100 a month in seats. If those agents lean on AI Drafts for 400 replies a month, the AI line is another $100 and the effective per-seat cost is $40, above Pro's headline rate. Model your draft volume before assuming AI is a rounding error.

## The export question

Data exports are a Pro-only feature. On a tool whose entire value is the accumulated history of your customer conversations, gating portability behind the most expensive tier is a meaningful lock-in mechanic, and it is worth resolving before you sign rather than after.

If you plan to stay on Standard or Plus, ask Groove in writing what you can retrieve and in what format. If the answer is unsatisfactory, that is a $23 per-user-per-month insurance premium you should price into the comparison.

## Who should and should not buy this

Groove fits small and mid-sized support teams that want a shared inbox done properly — fast, uncluttered, with real reporting — and do not want the configuration burden of a full service desk. At $12 a seat, Standard is one of the better-value entry points in the category for a team with one or two queues.

It fits poorly for organisations that need many queues on a budget, because inbox count drives you up the ladder quickly. It fits poorly for teams that need month-to-month flexibility, since none is offered. And it fits poorly where AI-generated replies are central to the workflow, because the per-credit model makes the highest-volume use case the most expensive one.

Groove revises tier contents periodically and AI credit pricing is newer than the seat pricing. Confirm current rates at groovehq.com before budgeting.
