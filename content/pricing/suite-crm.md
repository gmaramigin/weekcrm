## The licence is free; the deployment is not

SuiteCRM's pricing conversation has to start with a distinction that trips up almost every buyer: the *software* costs nothing, and the *CRM* costs something.

The software half is unambiguous. SuiteCRM is open source, free to download and run, with unlimited users, every module included and no feature restrictions. There is no community edition holding back the good parts — a fork of SugarCRM Community Edition, actively maintained for over a decade, and complete.

For a 50-person company, that is a genuinely radical proposition. The same headcount on Salesforce or Dynamics runs to tens of thousands of pounds a year. On SuiteCRM the licence line is zero, permanently.

What costs money is everything around it.

## The four line items

| Line item | Price | Who needs it |
|---|---|---|
| Software licence | £0 | Everyone |
| Managed hosting | From £50/mo | Anyone without a sysadmin |
| Standard Support | £1,200/yr (10 hrs) | Teams without in-house skills |
| SuiteASSURED | From £3,350/yr | Enterprises with legal review |
| Quick Start implementation | From £2,520 one-time | Most non-trivial deployments |

The hosting figure deserves emphasis because it is the strongest value on the page. **£50 a month, per deployment, for unlimited users** — with security, updates and backups managed. Most SaaS CRMs charge more than that for two seats. A 40-person sales organisation running fully managed SuiteCRM pays £600 a year in hosting, which is roughly what a single Salesforce Enterprise licence costs.

That structural difference — priced per deployment rather than per seat — is the entire economic argument for SuiteCRM, and it grows more compelling with every user added.

## Where the money actually goes

**Support is sold in thin allowances.** Standard Support is £1,200 a year for ten hours, or £120 per hour. That is a reasonable rate for expert help and a small quantity of it. During a data migration, a version upgrade or a difficult integration, ten hours is a morning's work. Treat it as a safety net rather than a substitute for internal capability, and assume you will either build skills in-house or retain a partner.

**SuiteASSURED is the enterprise price.** From £3,350 a year, it wraps warranty, indemnity and legal protections around the open-source software, with ten care hours included. Small businesses can ignore it. Organisations where procurement or legal review open-source usage generally cannot — and for those buyers, SuiteASSURED is the actual cost of SuiteCRM, which reframes it from "free CRM" to "£3,350+ CRM with unlimited users". Still competitive at scale, but a different conversation than the licence suggests.

**Implementation starts at £2,520** for a Quick Start engagement, quoted separately from hosting and support. Combine all three and a realistic first-year enterprise deployment is comfortably four figures before any custom development.

## The cost that never appears on an invoice

Self-hosting SuiteCRM is free in licence terms and expensive in attention. It is a substantial PHP application: server provisioning, PHP and database version management, scheduled jobs, mail configuration, backups, upgrade testing and security patching are all yours.

Organisations with a competent sysadmin absorb this comfortably and get an unlimited-user CRM for the cost of a virtual machine. Organisations without one tend to discover the gap at the worst moment — mid-upgrade, or after a vulnerability disclosure.

The £50/month managed hosting exists precisely to remove this, and for most buyers it is the correct choice. Paying £600 a year to make server administration someone else's problem is not a close call when the alternative is unplanned engineering time.

## A note on currency

All SuiteCRM pricing is published in GBP. Buyers outside the UK take on exchange-rate exposure across multi-year commitments, and the effective cost of hosting and SuiteASSURED moves with the currency. Minor at £50 a month; more relevant against a £3,350 annual assurance package.

## Who should and should not buy this

SuiteCRM fits organisations with many CRM users and constrained budgets — 30, 50, 200 people where per-seat SaaS pricing becomes punitive. It also fits teams that need deep customisation and data ownership, and public-sector or regulated buyers who require on-premise deployment. Managed hosting at £50/month for unlimited users is the standout offer in this market segment.

It fits badly for small teams wanting polish out of the box: a five-person company will get a better daily experience from Pipedrive or HubSpot's free tier for less total effort, because SuiteCRM's economics only start to dominate at scale. It also fits badly for teams with no technical capability and no partner budget — free software with no one to run it is not a saving.
