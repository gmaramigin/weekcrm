## A cheap headline attached to a modular system

Commence CRM advertises entry at $12 per user per month, which places it among the least expensive CRMs with a real feature list. That number is accurate and, in isolation, misleading — for two reasons that compound.

The first is what Essentials actually contains. At $12 you get accounts, contacts, segmentation, activity tracking, tasks, notes, calendar, lead tracking and reporting. What you do not get is opportunity tracking. No pipeline, no forecast, no deal stages. Essentials is a well-built contact manager; it is not a sales CRM, and any team that intends to manage deals starts at Sales for $19.

The second is that Commence is modular, and the modules are where the money is.

## What the modules do to the bill

Marketing, Customer Service and Project Management are separate add-ons at $12, $12 and $10 per user per month. Critically, they are priced **per user across the account**, not per user who uses them.

| Configuration | Per user/month | 10-user team |
|---|---|---|
| Essentials only | $12 | $120/mo |
| Sales CRM | $19 | $190/mo |
| Sales + Marketing | $31 | $310/mo |
| Enterprise, all three modules | $66 | $660/mo |

That bottom row is the one to sit with. A fully configured Commence seat is $66 per user per month — five and a half times the advertised entry price, and comparable to HubSpot Sales Professional. Commence is not, at that configuration, a budget product; it simply enters the conversation as one.

The per-user module pricing is the sharpest edge. If two people in a ten-person company handle support tickets, you still pay $120/month for the Customer Service module, because it is billed across all ten seats. There is no partial adoption. Modules are an account-level decision priced as a per-seat one, and that asymmetry is where budgets get away from teams.

## Where the tiers earn their place

**Essentials to Sales** is not a real choice for most buyers — it is the price of admission. Pipeline and forecasting are table stakes, and $7 more per user is a fair price for them. Assume $19 is the floor.

**Sales to Enterprise** costs $13 more and buys workflow automation, custom fields and views, role-based access, quoting and pricebook, and Outlook/Gmail sync. The email sync placement is aggressive: most competitors treat two-way mail sync as a mid-tier feature, and putting it in the top tier is a meaningful push upward for teams that live in Outlook. If you need custom fields or automation, Enterprise is unavoidable, and most growing teams need both within a year.

**The Project Management module** at $10 is the best-value add-on in the lineup, because the honest alternative is a separate tool and a synchronisation problem. Marketing and Customer Service are more contestable — both face strong standalone competitors, and both carry the full per-seat penalty.

## Terms worth negotiating

Everything published is billed annually. There is no advertised monthly option, which means the real first cheque for a ten-person Sales CRM deployment is $2,280. Ask directly whether monthly exists and what the premium is; the absence from the pricing page is itself informative.

The three-user minimum is mild by category standards — $36/month at Essentials is not a barrier — but it does mean solo operators are paying for company they do not have.

## Who should and should not buy this

Commence suits small and mid-sized B2B teams of roughly five to fifty that want one vendor covering CRM plus one adjacent function, and that value configurability over polish. Sales CRM at $19 is genuinely competitive for what it includes, and Enterprise at $32 undercuts most rivals offering comparable customisation.

It disappoints two profiles. Teams drawn in by the $12 figure discover that a working sales CRM costs 58% more, and a configured one with modules costs several times that. And organisations where only a subset of staff need a given module pay for everyone regardless — the pricing model actively penalises exactly the mixed-function teams that modular software is supposed to serve.
