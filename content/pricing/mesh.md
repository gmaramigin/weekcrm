## A well-priced personal product with a very low team ceiling

Mesh prices its individual tiers sensibly and then does something unusual at the team level: it caps the team plan at four people.

Not four to start. Four, maximum. The fifth member triggers an Enterprise negotiation.

That single constraint shapes everything about how this pricing should be read, because it means the published rates describe individuals and very small groups only — and stop describing your costs entirely at a headcount most companies reach early.

## The individual tiers are the strong part

For one person, Mesh is priced well.

**Personal (Free)** carries up to 1,000 contacts with the full feature set — email, calendar and social imports, search, groups, reconnect prompts, reminders, birthday notifications, unlimited life updates. Nothing important is held back except scale.

**Pro at $10/month** removes the contact ceiling and adds spreadsheet and CSV imports, real-time network refreshes and priority support.

Ten dollars for an unlimited personal CRM with real enrichment is competitive — cheaper than Dex, cheaper than Clay's paid tiers, and cheaper than Relate's $15 Personal Pro.

The free plan's honest weakness is that 1,000 contacts is not many once the product does its job. Mesh's whole value proposition is importing everything — every email correspondent, every calendar attendee, every social connection — and a professional with several years of accumulated correspondence typically blows past 1,000 during the first sync. Free is less a plan you settle on than a trial that happens not to expire.

## The four-member wall

| Plan | Price | Members | Contacts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personal | $0 | 1 | 1,000 |
| Pro | $10/mo | 1 | Unlimited |
| Team | $40/seat/mo | Up to 4 | Unlimited |
| Enterprise | Custom | Unlimited | Unlimited |

Two things happen at once when you move from Pro to Team, and both deserve scrutiny.

The **rate quadruples**, from $10 to $40 per person. Mesh justifies this with advanced data enrichment, custom integrations and admin tools — genuine capabilities, not a collaboration surcharge. But it means two colleagues who simply want to share a network pay $80/month, against $20 for two unconnected Pro accounts. The premium for working together is 4x, and there is no lighter shared tier.

The **ceiling is four**. This is the part with no real precedent. Most vendors' small-team plans accommodate 10 or 25 users; Mesh's stops at four. A five-person agency, a small investment team, a founding team with a couple of hires — all of them are outside published pricing and into a custom quote.

For a prospective buyer this is the number to plan around. If there is any prospect of a fifth person within your planning horizon, the relevant price is not $40 — it is whatever Enterprise turns out to cost, and you should establish that before adopting the product rather than after building your network inside it.

## Security is a pricing tier

SOC 2 Type II certification and SAML SSO are Enterprise-only features.

That is a familiar arrangement but it bites harder here because of the four-member cap. A three-person team at a company with a standard security review cannot buy Team at any price — the compliance requirement alone forces Enterprise, independent of headcount. Two separate gates, either sufficient, both leading to the same custom quote.

## No annual discount

Every published Mesh rate is monthly. No annual pricing is advertised at any tier.

This is genuinely unusual — the 15–25% annual discount is close to universal in this market — and it is worth raising with sales directly if you plan to commit. Its absence may simply reflect a small vendor keeping pricing simple, but it also means there is no lever to pull on cost the way there is with most competitors.

## Who should and should not buy this

Mesh is a strong buy for individuals. Pro at $10/month for unlimited contacts with real-time network refreshes is among the best-value personal CRMs available, and the free tier is a legitimate way to test whether the import-everything model suits how you work.

It is a considered buy for pairs and trios who need shared enrichment and can absorb $40 a seat.

It is a poor fit for anything larger, or for anyone in an organisation with security requirements — in both cases the published pricing simply does not apply, and the real cost is unknown until you ask. Given how much of Mesh's value comes from accumulated network data, discovering the Enterprise price after migrating years of contacts is a weak negotiating position. Ask first.
