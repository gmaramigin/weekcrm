## You buy capabilities, not seats

Nearly every business platform charges per user for access to everything, then tiers the everything. BlueCamroo inverts that: it assigns User Roles to people and charges for the roles. A salesperson who only touches CRM costs less than an operations manager who touches CRM, projects and cases.

The company's own framing is direct — pay only for the feature each user needs — and the model has one genuinely unusual consequence at the small end. CRM-only User Roles are free for up to four users. Not a limited trial tier: four people running lead, contact, opportunity and account management at no cost.

## Where the model wins and loses

The deciding variable is how specialised your team is.

A ten-person consultancy where four people are client-facing sales, three are delivery, and three are management pays for exactly that distribution. On a flat $45-per-seat competitor, the same company pays $450 a month regardless of the fact that seven of those people use a third of the product.

A ten-person team where every single person needs CRM, projects, cases and reporting pays for ten full role sets — around $410 to $490 — plus the ongoing work of administering role assignments. Against a flat competitor at the same rate, that is a worse deal, because you have bought identical access and added overhead.

Run the shape of your team, not the size, before deciding.

## The staged adoption path

The free four-user CRM tier makes a specific rollout pattern possible that most platforms do not support: start as a CRM, add other functional areas later, and pay only as each area comes online.

That is genuinely useful for a growing business. You can implement CRM first with a small team at zero cost, prove the workflow, then bring project management in when you actually need it and pay for the project roles at that point. Most competitors force the full platform decision on day one.

The corresponding risk is drift. Roles accumulate — someone needs project visibility for one engagement and keeps the role for two years. Without a periodic audit, a role-based bill grows in a way a seat-based bill does not, and nobody notices because no new people were hired.

## The add-ons

Storage and email marketing are priced separately from role licences. Storage is the predictable kind of add-on. Email marketing is not: for a business running regular campaigns to a large list, that line can exceed the CRM roles entirely, and it should be quoted alongside the licences rather than discovered later.

## What to work out before you buy

Three questions determine your BlueCamroo price, and none of them is headcount.

Which functional areas does each person genuinely need? Not which would be nice — which they will use weekly. How many people can sit inside the free four-user CRM allocation? And what is your email marketing volume, since that add-on is priced independently of everything else?

Answer those three and the quote follows. Skip them and you will be quoted for a full-role deployment that costs more than the model was designed to.

## Who should and should not buy this

BlueCamroo fits small and mid-sized businesses with clearly differentiated roles — agencies, consultancies, professional services firms — that want CRM, projects and case management on one platform without paying full price for every capability for every person. The free four-user CRM tier makes it particularly attractive for a business starting with sales and expanding later.

It fits poorly for teams where everyone does everything, which is common in very small companies and startups; there the role model adds administration without saving money. It also fits poorly for organisations that want a simple, predictable per-seat number for budgeting, since role-based pricing is configuration-dependent by design.

BlueCamroo revises role definitions and add-on pricing periodically. Confirm current role rates and the free-tier limit at bluecamroo.com before budgeting.
