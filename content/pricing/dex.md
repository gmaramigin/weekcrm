## A simple price with one expensive decision

Dex's pricing is about as uncomplicated as software gets: one main plan, one higher tier for heavy users, a student discount. The only decision that costs real money is billing frequency, and it costs more than most people expect.

Annual is $12 a month, or $144 a year. Monthly is $20, or $240 a year. That is a 67% premium for the right to cancel, against a category norm of 20 to 25%. Dex is effectively pricing month-to-month as a trial extension rather than a genuine option, and the arithmetic says decide within the trial rather than hedging for six months and paying $48 extra for the privilege.

## What you are actually paying for

The feature list reads like a personal CRM checklist — mobile, web and desktop, Map View, custom fields, contact import — but the thing that justifies a subscription rather than a spreadsheet is the sync layer. Two-way contact sync, messaging and email integrations, and LinkedIn sync for up to 2,500 connections mean the database maintains itself. Nobody sustains a manual personal CRM. That is the whole product thesis, and it is why the price is per month rather than a one-time purchase.

It also defines the failure mode. If your relationships live somewhere Dex does not sync from, the automatic half disappears and you are left paying for a contact manager with a map in it.

Most people should start on Premium and never leave. The two triggers for upgrading are concrete rather than aspirational: your LinkedIn network exceeds the sync ceiling, or you need to send batched personal outreach. Recruiters, VCs, community builders and business-development people hit both. A founder keeping track of ninety important relationships hits neither.

## Where Dex sits against the alternatives

At $144 a year, Dex is priced below almost every sales CRM and above every notes app. That is the right position for what it does, but it means the comparison set is unusual: it is not competing with a CRM, it is competing with your inbox, a spreadsheet, and the honest possibility of doing nothing.

Against a spreadsheet, the sync layer wins outright — a personal CRM that requires manual upkeep is abandoned within a month. Against a sales CRM, Dex loses every collaboration and pipeline feature and wins on the thing sales CRMs are bad at, which is remembering people you are not currently selling to.

## Who should and should not buy this

Dex fits people whose professional life runs on a network rather than a pipeline: founders, investors, consultants, recruiters, anyone whose next opportunity comes from a conversation they had two years ago. At $12 a month it is a rounding error against the value of one remembered follow-up.

It fits poorly for teams. There is no shared workspace, no collaboration, no team CRM functionality at all, so a pair of co-founders each pay full price and still work in separate silos. It also fits poorly for anyone who wants deal stages, forecasting or reporting — that is a different category of product, and Dex has deliberately not built toward it.
