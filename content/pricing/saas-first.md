## One plan, and a meter beside it

SAAS First has done something unusual twice over. First, it sells a single plan — no Starter, no Pro, no Enterprise. Every module (omnichannel inbox, AI chatbot, customer tracking, CRM, boards, analytics, event tracking, marketing campaigns) is in the $9 per member per month price.

Second, it prices its AI at exactly the value of the seat. Each member's $9 comes with 100 credits, described as worth $9. Extra credits are $0.09 each — the same unit rate, no premium for topping up and no discount for volume.

Read those two facts together and the model becomes clear: the platform is priced at roughly zero and the AI is the product. That is a defensible bet in 2026, and it means your evaluation should start with conversation volume rather than headcount.

## What a credit buys

One credit equals one chatbot conversation inside a 24-hour window, regardless of how many messages that conversation contains. That is a customer-friendly definition — a twelve-message troubleshooting exchange costs the same as a one-line question — and it makes forecasting straightforward, because you can model on unique conversations rather than message volume.

| Monthly conversations | 5-member team credits included | Extra credits needed | Credit cost | Seat cost | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 300 | 500 | 0 | $0 | $45 | $45 |
| 1,000 | 500 | 500 | $45 | $45 | $90 |
| 5,000 | 500 | 4,500 | $405 | $45 | $450 |
| 20,000 | 500 | 19,500 | $1,755 | $45 | $1,800 |

The seat column stops mattering almost immediately. By 5,000 conversations, seats are 10% of the bill; by 20,000 they are 2.5%.

## The allowance is attached to the wrong variable

This is the structural quirk worth understanding. Credits are granted per member, but consumed per customer conversation — and those two numbers have nothing to do with each other.

A two-person startup with a popular product gets 200 included conversations and may need 3,000. A ten-person agency with a quiet website gets 1,000 included and uses 150, wasting 850 credits' worth of allowance every month.

Neither is a flaw exactly; it is just a mismatch between the meter and the driver. The practical response is to price SAAS First on conversations first and treat seats as a small fixed overhead, rather than the other way round.

## Where it sits against the category

Customer engagement platforms have converged on charging for AI resolutions — Intercom's Fin being the reference point — and SAAS First is doing the same thing at a much lower unit price. Nine cents a conversation is well below the per-resolution rates common in the category.

What you give up for that is scale infrastructure: no enterprise tier, no negotiated committed-use rate, no volume discount curve, and a single plan that cannot be tailored. For a company doing 50,000 conversations a month, the absence of a volume rate is a real cost — that is $4,500 at list with nothing to negotiate against.

## Who should and should not buy this

SAAS First fits small SaaS companies, startups and lean support teams that want an inbox, a CRM, analytics and an AI chatbot in one place without assembling four subscriptions. At $9 a seat with every module included, the platform cost is close to irrelevant, which is a genuinely strong position for a team watching every line item.

It fits poorly for high-volume consumer support, where the lack of a volume credit rate means you pay list price on every one of tens of thousands of conversations. It also fits poorly for organisations that need procurement levers — a single published plan leaves nothing to negotiate — or that require enterprise compliance packaging, which a one-plan product does not offer.

Use the 14-day trial for what it is best at: measuring real credit consumption on live traffic. That number, not the $9, decides whether this is cheap for you.
