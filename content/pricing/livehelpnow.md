## A modest help desk with an expensive AI layer bolted on

LiveHelpNow's core pricing is unremarkable in a good way. Essential from $39 a month, Professional from $59, Premium from $79 — three tiers, sensibly separated, competitive against Freshdesk and Zendesk for a small support operation.

Then there is the AI rate card, and it operates on an entirely different scale:

- AI Agent Assist — **included free**
- Knowledge AI — **from $99/month**
- Conversational AI — **from $499/month**
- Conversational AI Plus — **from $799/month**

Conversational AI costs roughly eight times the Professional plan it attaches to. For most buyers, the question "what does LiveHelpNow cost?" is really "do we want the AI?", because that single decision swings the bill by an order of magnitude.

## Read "from" carefully

Every published figure carries a "from" prefix. This is not decoration — LiveHelpNow's pricing scales with agent count and usage, and the rate card describes entry points rather than quotes.

That has a practical consequence for evaluation. Competitors like Freshdesk and Help Scout publish clean per-agent rates that let you multiply by headcount and compare. LiveHelpNow's numbers cannot be used that way. A five-agent desk and a fifteen-agent desk both start "from $59" on Professional and will be invoiced very differently.

Get a written quote for your actual agent count before drawing any conclusion about whether this is cheaper than the alternatives. The rate card is a floor, not a forecast.

## Essential is chat-only

The gap between $39 and $59 is one feature, and it is a decisive one: **ticketing starts at Professional.**

Essential covers live chat, a knowledge base, a support portal, unlimited articles, surveys, canned responses, secure forms and social channels. That is a capable chat product. What it cannot do is manage an email queue.

Almost every support team handles both. Customers who cannot get an answer in chat send email, and a desk without ticketing loses them. So while $39 is the advertised entry point, the realistic entry point for a working support operation is $59 — which also brings performance dashboards, KPI reporting and data export, without which the desk cannot be measured.

Premium at $79 adds white-glove onboarding, a monthly success call, priority support and — notably — removal of LiveHelpNow branding from the widget. For consumer-facing brands and agencies presenting to clients, that last item frequently decides the tier on its own.

## The AI economics

The free AI Agent Assist is a smart piece of packaging. It helps agents find and draft answers, which is a real productivity gain and costs nothing. It also deflects exactly zero tickets, because it never speaks to a customer.

Deflection is what the paid tiers sell, and they are metered by volume rather than seats:

| Add-on | From | Included volume (Professional / Premium) |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge AI | $99/mo | 2,000 / 5,000 AI answers |
| Conversational AI | $499/mo | 1,000 / 3,000 conversations |
| Conversational AI Plus | $799/mo | 7,000+ conversations |

Conversational AI works out to roughly $0.50 per conversation at the entry allowance. Whether that is good value depends entirely on volume and on what an agent-handled conversation costs you. If a deflected conversation saves five minutes of agent time, the maths is comfortable at scale and poor below it.

The critical structural point is that **AI cost tracks customer demand, not your headcount**. A marketing campaign, an outage or a seasonal spike drives conversation volume upward, and the AI bill follows — pushing you toward the $799 tier or a Premium upgrade for reasons entirely outside your control. That is a different risk profile from per-agent pricing, and it should be modelled against your busiest month rather than your average one.

For most small and mid-sized desks, Knowledge AI at $99 is the sensible starting point: it improves self-service answer quality without committing to conversational automation at five times the price.

## Who should and should not buy this

LiveHelpNow fits chat-led support teams that want live chat, a knowledge base and ticketing from one vendor at a modest base cost, particularly those able to start on Professional and add Knowledge AI later. The free AI Agent Assist is a genuine benefit that competitors charge for.

It fits badly for buyers who need transparent per-agent pricing to compare vendors, since the "from" rates make like-for-like comparison impossible without a sales conversation. It also fits badly for teams that want conversational AI on a small budget — at $499 a month the automation layer costs many times the help desk, and desks with modest volume will not recover it.
