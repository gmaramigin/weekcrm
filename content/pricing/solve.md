## A pricing page with nothing to hide and nothing to climb

Most CRM pricing analysis is archaeology: working out which of five tiers actually contains the feature you need, then discovering it is the fourth one. Solve CRM makes that work unnecessary. There is one plan, Standard, at $39 per user per month, and it contains the entire product — full web application, full iOS application, no asterisks.

Solve markets this as "no hidden tiers", and the claim holds. There is no Professional plan dangling reporting, no Enterprise tier holding the API hostage. Whatever Solve does, $39 buys.

That honesty makes the one constraint stand out sharply.

## The four-seat floor

Solve requires a minimum of four users. That is the whole of the fine print, and it reshapes who this product is for.

| Actual users | Seats billed | Monthly cost | Real cost per person |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | $156 | $156 |
| 2 | 4 | $156 | $78 |
| 4 | 4 | $156 | $39 |
| 12 | 12 | $468 | $39 |

A solo consultant evaluating Solve is not looking at a $39 CRM. They are looking at a $156 one — four times the advertised rate, and more expensive per head than Salesforce Enterprise. Two-person practices pay double the sticker.

The advertised price only becomes the actual price at exactly four users, and this is the single number that should drive the buy decision. Below four, Solve is categorically the wrong choice and there is no negotiating around it. At four and above, the pricing is clean and predictable in a way very little else in this market is.

## What single-tier pricing costs you

Flat pricing is a genuine feature, but it has a symmetric downside worth naming: there is no cheap seat.

Tiered vendors let you put an occasional user — a bookkeeper who checks invoices monthly, a part-time office manager, an advisor who reads but never writes — on a $12 starter plan or a free viewer licence. Solve has no such option. Every login is $39, whether it is used every hour or twice a quarter.

For a tight team of full-time users that is irrelevant. For a practice with a long tail of light-touch people who need occasional visibility, it inflates the bill in a way a tiered competitor would not, and it quietly pushes teams toward sharing logins — which is worse for both security and audit trail.

There is also no advertised annual discount. Giving up a year of flexibility normally buys 15–20% back; here, monthly pricing appears to simply be the pricing. Ask sales before assuming otherwise.

## Where it earns the money

At $39 all-in, Solve is priced against the mid-tier of the market — roughly where Pipedrive Advanced, Copper Professional and Insightly Plus sit — while giving you the top of its own range. For professional-services firms the relevant comparison is not really other CRMs but a CRM plus a lightweight project tool, since Solve carries client and project workflow natively rather than treating the deal as the terminal object.

That is the case for the price. A consultancy tracking engagements past the close — deliverables, client files, recurring work — gets one system where the alternative is two subscriptions and a synchronisation problem.

## Who should and should not buy this

Solve fits professional-services teams of four to twenty-five: consultancies, agencies, accounting practices, anyone managing ongoing client relationships rather than one-shot transactions. In that band the pricing is simple, the four-seat minimum is invisible, and the included project layer is doing real work.

It is the wrong tool for solo operators and pairs, where the minimum makes it two to four times more expensive per person than it looks. It is also a poor fit for teams with many light-touch users, since every one of them costs full freight.

Check solve360.com for current pricing and confirm storage limits in writing before migrating client documents.
