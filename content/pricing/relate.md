## Free where it counts, expensive where it matters

Relate's pricing does something interesting: it gives away the resource every competitor meters, and charges for the one most people assume is free.

Contacts are unlimited on the free plan. Not 500, not 1,000 — unlimited, alongside LinkedIn integration, import/export and 250 enrichments. Against personal CRMs that cap free tiers at a few hundred records, this is conspicuously generous.

What Relate does cap is **history**. Email and note history is limited to 30 days on every free plan. Older correspondence rolls off.

For a relationship CRM, that is the load-bearing constraint. The product's entire premise is remembering context about people — what you discussed, when, what was promised. A 30-day window keeps the contact and discards the relationship. You can hold a hundred thousand names and still be unable to recall what you said to any of them last quarter.

Personal Pro at $15/month exists almost entirely to remove that cap. You are not buying features; you are buying permanence.

## The individual-to-team cliff

Relate's two paid tracks are priced four times apart.

| Plan | Price | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| Personal Free | $0 | Network keeping, 30-day memory |
| Personal Pro | $15/mo | Same, with permanent history |
| Team Free | $0 | Shared, still 30-day memory |
| Team Business | $60/user/mo | Deals, reporting, dashboards |
| Enterprise | Custom | Data warehouse, reverse ETL, multi-workspace |

The jump from $15 to $60 per user is among the steepest individual-to-team transitions in this market, and there is nothing in between. A three-person firm that simply wants shared contacts with history that does not evaporate has two options: run three separate Personal Pro accounts at $45/month and lose shared visibility, or pay $180/month for Business and receive pipeline reporting it may not want.

That gap is the clearest weakness in the pricing. Business is built for a sales team — unlimited qualified deals, reporting, dashboards — and small collaborative groups who want the personal product with shared history are pushed into paying for sales machinery to get it.

## Enrichments do not scale with price

This detail is easy to miss and expensive to ignore: **250 enrichments per plan, at every tier.**

Free gets 250. Personal Pro gets 250. Team Business — at $60 per user per month — also gets 250, with pay-as-you-go beyond.

The allowance is flat while the price is per-seat. A ten-person Business team paying $600/month receives the same enrichment quota as somebody on the free plan, which makes overage charges not a risk but a near-certainty for any team using enrichment as part of its workflow.

If enrichment is central to how you would use Relate, price it as a separate line item and get the per-enrichment rate in writing. The seat cost is the predictable half of the bill; this is the other half.

## Guests are metered too

Guest invites run 10 on free plans, 50 on paid, 100 on Enterprise. For a venture firm, agency or advisory business that routinely shares network access with portfolio companies, partners or clients, that ceiling is a real constraint and it arrives independently of seat count. Worth checking against how you actually intend to collaborate.

## What Enterprise signals

The Enterprise tier's contents — data warehouse integration, reverse ETL, multi-workspaces, custom integrations — tell you who Relate is ultimately built for. Reverse ETL is not a feature small businesses ask for. It is what a company with a data team and an existing warehouse needs to push enriched relationship data back into its own systems.

That is a considered target: firms where the network *is* the asset and needs to be operationalised, not just browsed.

## Who should and should not buy this

Relate suits individuals and network-driven professionals — investors, founders, advisors, business developers — where the free plan is genuinely useful and Personal Pro at $15 is an easy call once you want permanent memory. It also suits sales teams that want a relationship-first CRM with real enrichment, provided they budget for enrichment overage separately.

It fits badly for small collaborative teams that want shared history without pipeline reporting: the missing tier between $15 and $60 leaves them overpaying or working around the product. And any team enriching heavily should treat the flat 250-enrichment allowance as the main pricing risk, not the seat rate.
