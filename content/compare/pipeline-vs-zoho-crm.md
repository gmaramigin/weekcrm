## TL;DR

- Zoho is cheaper at every comparable tier and free for three users forever. Pipeline CRM starts at $25/user and has no free plan.
- Price is not why teams pick Pipeline CRM. They pick it because it does one thing, US-based phone support is included on every plan, and nobody has to configure anything.
- Zoho's real cost is weeks of someone's attention. Its licence fees stop being the interesting variable somewhere around the second week of implementation.

## Two philosophies about who does the work

Pipeline CRM makes decisions for you. One product shape — deal and lead tracking, native email drip campaigns, task automation, dashboards — with capacity limits that tell you which tier you belong on. A team can be running properly inside a week, and if something breaks you call a person in the US who answers.

Zoho hands you the toolkit: custom modules for objects that are not leads and deals, Blueprint for enforcing process stages, validation rules, multi-pipeline logic, sandboxes. Nothing stops you from building something incoherent. Teams that succeed with Zoho put someone in charge of it for a few weeks; teams that treat it as sign-up-and-go end up with a half-built system everyone routes around.

Be honest about whether you have that person. It settles this comparison faster than any feature table.

## Pricing

Pipeline CRM: Start $25, Develop $33, Grow $49 per user annually, monthly running 16–20% higher. Tiers gate on capacity rather than capability — 1 pipeline and 250 active deals on Start, 2 and 2,500 on Develop, 5 and unlimited on Grow. The deal cap counts across the whole account.

Zoho CRM: free for three users permanently, then Standard $14, Professional $23, Enterprise $40, Ultimate $52 per user annually. Monthly billing adds 25–43%. No seat minimum.

The comparison that matters is not $25 against $14. It is Pipeline CRM Develop at $33 against Zoho Enterprise at $40, because that is where each becomes the thing people recommend. At ten users, $3,960 versus $4,800 a year — close enough to decide on fit, not cost.

## What each vendor locks behind an upgrade

Pipeline CRM's most-requested gate is email automation, which lives in Grow at $49 — 96% above the headline. If you plan to run outbound sequences from inside the CRM, that is your price, and the alternative of a separate sales engagement tool usually costs more once integration overhead is counted.

Zoho's gate is Zia, its AI assistant, plus custom modules and the deeper customization layer, all at Enterprise ($40). Anyone comparing Zoho's $14 entry price against AI-equipped competitors is comparing the wrong tier. Zoho also enforces per-edition ceilings on workflow rule counts, custom field counts, and API calls that are easy to hit without warning.

The add-on picture differs too. Pipeline CRM sells three: data enrichment ($19–$69/month), email validation ($25 flat), and Pipeline Connect managed integrations ($99–$249/month), the last of which deserves a challenge. Zoho charges no mandatory onboarding fee at all, unlike HubSpot's $1,500; the cost is internal time rather than an invoice line.

## Depth, and whether you will use it

Zoho's ceiling is high: multi-currency, custom modules, Blueprint process enforcement, deal predictions and anomaly detection through Zia, and native connections into Desk, Books, Campaigns, and Sign. Against Salesforce Enterprise at $175/user, Zoho Enterprise at $40 delivers broadly comparable configurability for roughly a quarter, and a company can grow into complexity without a forced migration later.

Pipeline CRM's ceiling is lower and it says so. Advanced forecasting and AI tooling are limited, reporting lacks the granularity larger sales operations need, and the interface is functional rather than modern. For a team in construction, manufacturing, or distribution that wants to track deals and stop thinking about it, none of that is a problem. Zoho's interface draws the same criticism from a different direction: capable, improved, still lagging Attio and HubSpot on feel.

## The ecosystem question

If you already run Zoho Mail, Books, or Desk, this comparison is largely settled. Zoho One at $37/user bundles 45+ applications and undercuts Zoho CRM Enterprise as a standalone purchase — excellent value if you use several apps, and 44 unopened applications if you do not. Pipeline CRM has no ecosystem play: native integrations plus Zapier, and that is the extent of the ambition. For a business with a settled tool stack, that is a reasonable position.

## Who should pick what

- **Founder plus two salespeople with no budget** → Zoho's free tier. Three seats, permanently, with real lead and deal management. Nothing else here comes close.
- **SMB sales teams wanting a CRM running by Friday** → Pipeline CRM Develop at $33, with phone support included.
- **Cost-sensitive teams with someone willing to own configuration** → Zoho Enterprise at $40. The depth is the point and it is where the depth lives.
- **Companies already using other Zoho apps** → Zoho, and price Zoho One before buying CRM standalone.
- **Teams building an outbound motion** → price Pipeline CRM Grow at $49 against Zoho Enterprise at $40 with its automation layer; Zoho generally wins that matchup.
- **International teams needing multi-currency and custom objects** → Zoho. Pipeline CRM is not built for that shape.

## Bottom line

Zoho wins on paper by a comfortable margin — cheaper, deeper, free at the bottom, and with an ecosystem that keeps paying off if you use it. What it charges instead is implementation attention, and plenty of teams underestimate that until they are four weeks into a half-built system. Pipeline CRM's argument is the absence of that risk: three transparent tiers, a phone number that reaches a human, and a product narrow enough that there is no wrong way to set it up. Count your open opportunities against Pipeline CRM's caps, and be realistic about who would own a Zoho rollout. Those two answers decide it.
