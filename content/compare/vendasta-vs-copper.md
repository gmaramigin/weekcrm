## TL;DR

- **Vendasta** sells agencies a business model: a white-label marketplace of 250+ resellable marketing products, client portals, billing, and a sales pipeline, all under your brand. Tiers are $99, $499, and $999 a month for platform access.
- **Copper** sells agencies a working habit: a CRM that lives inside Gmail, Calendar, and Drive, auto-building contact records from email history. $23/seat Basic, $59 Professional, $99 Business on annual billing.
- Vendasta is priced per platform. Copper is priced per person. Neither pricing page tells the full story, but for different reasons.
- If you want to resell SEO and reputation management under your logo, only one of these does that. If you want your account managers to stop losing follow-ups, only the other one does that.

## They are not really competitors

The reason both names show up on agency shortlists is that "agency software" is a category people search for, not a category that exists. Vendasta's job is fulfillment and resale — you sell listings management, reputation monitoring, SEO, ads, and websites to local businesses without staffing any of those disciplines, and Vendasta supplies the product, the client-facing portal, and the invoice. The CRM inside it exists to track the deals that feed that machine.

Copper has no fulfillment layer at all. Its job is that a rep or account manager working in Gmail never leaves Gmail: contacts and deals appear in a sidebar, records enrich themselves from email and calendar history, Copper GPT answers questions about your data in plain language, and there's a light project management layer for post-sale work. It is a relationship tool for service businesses.

An agency could plausibly run both. Plenty do.

## Two pricing models with two different traps

Vendasta's tiers are platform access fees, and the wholesale cost of every product a client consumes is billed separately — usually the larger number by a wide margin. Twenty clients each buying listings management and reputation monitoring will cost far more in wholesale product spend than the $99 or $499 subscription. There's also a signing fee equal to one month at every tier ($99, $499, $999), and payment processing at 2.9% + $0.30 through the platform's Stripe-powered billing, which at $50,000 a month in client payments is roughly $1,450 — more than a Professional subscription.

The Starter-to-Professional step is 404% with nothing in between, and Professional and Premium both carry twelve-month commitments. Only Starter is genuinely month-to-month.

Copper's trap is quieter. Basic at $23 covers Gmail sync, contacts, and one pipeline, and omits Opportunities and Leads entirely; workflow automation, multiple pipelines, and most integrations start at Professional. Copper's own pricing page notes 63% of customers start at Professional, so treat $59 as the floor. There is no light or read-only seat, so an ops lead who checks closed-won totals monthly costs the same as a closer.

At five seats Professional is $295 a month; at twenty it's $1,180. Compare that to Vendasta Starter's flat $99 and the platform fee looks cheap — until you remember it doesn't include the products.

## Where each one is genuinely strong

Vendasta's Snapshot Reports are a real sales asset: automated digital audits you can put in front of a local business to start a conversation. The white-labeling goes deep — portals, dashboards, and client communication all carry your brand. And the built-in subscription billing removes back-office work that would otherwise scale linearly with client count.

Copper's strength is adoption. CRMs fail because nobody logs in, and a CRM that is already inside the inbox sidesteps that in a way no amount of training does. Auto data capture from email and calendar means records stay current without discipline. The AI tooling — email rewriter, template generator, LinkedIn email finder in beta — is useful rather than decorative.

## The honest weaknesses

Vendasta's breadth overwhelms small teams; it's built for agencies operating at volume, and the mandatory onboarding fee plus the annual commitment on Professional make it an uncomfortable place to experiment. The deeper structural issue is that your product quality is Vendasta's product quality and your margin is capped by their wholesale price. If your differentiation is the work itself rather than catalog breadth, that's a bad trade.

Copper's constraint is simpler: it's a Google product. On Microsoft 365 the integration that justifies the price disappears and you're left with a mid-tier CRM. Reporting and forecasting are competent but shallower than Salesforce or Pipedrive for complex sales orgs, and marketing tooling is thin enough that most teams pay for Mailchimp alongside it. Migration off Copper is also messier than average, because records auto-created from email history need cleanup that a hand-maintained CRM doesn't.

## Who should pick what

- **Agencies building recurring revenue by reselling marketing services to local SMBs** → Vendasta. Start on Starter, prove the margin against real clients, and only sign Professional when the client count supports $6,000 a year in platform cost alone.
- **Agencies and consultancies of 5–40 people all-in on Google Workspace** → Copper Professional. It gets used, which is the hard part.
- **Media companies and ISVs wanting a branded software storefront** → Vendasta, and no realistic alternative here.
- **Agencies whose differentiation is craft, not catalog** → Copper, plus whatever specialist tools you already trust.
- **Any team split across Gmail and Outlook** → not Copper. You'd be paying for an inbox integration half your seats can't use.

## Bottom line

Ask what problem you're actually funding. Vendasta is capital expenditure on a revenue model — you're buying the ability to sell services you don't deliver, and the arithmetic that matters is retail minus wholesale times client count, not the tier price. Copper is operating expenditure on sales hygiene, and the arithmetic that matters is $59 times the number of people who genuinely need a login. Verify current terms at vendasta.com/pricing and copper.com/pricing before committing to either, especially the twelve-month terms on Vendasta's upper tiers.
