## TL;DR

- Sprinklr's self-serve plan ended on 30 April 2026. Every new deployment is an enterprise contract, with benchmarks putting entry near $50,000 a year and median annual contract value around $129,000.
- Kustomer's Ultimate plan lists at $139 per seat per month, annual billing only, with an eight-seat minimum — a $13,344 floor.
- Both unbundle AI. Kustomer meters customer-facing AI at $0.60 per engaged conversation and charges $40 per rep for agent-side AI. Sprinklr rolls AI into the contract but prices partly on data volume.
- If your budget is under six figures, this is not a two-way comparison anymore. Sprinklr removed itself.

## The floor difference is the headline

Start with what it takes to become a customer. Kustomer requires eight seats on an annual contract: $13,344 before AI, $17,184 with AI for Reps switched on. That is a serious commitment but a legible one, and a 20-agent consumer brand can model it in a spreadsheet.

Sprinklr no longer has a comparable entry. Its $199-per-seat self-serve plan (or $249 monthly) closed to new buyers and expired for existing ones at the end of April 2026. Benchmark contract values now run from roughly $26,000 to over $500,000, with implementation adding 15–30% of contract value and training billed separately at $10,000–$20,000. A $100,000 contract realistically lands at $125,000–$150,000 in year one.

Practically, a 30-agent support team that could once have bought Sprinklr for about $72,000 a year cannot buy it at all. That is a deliberate strategic choice by the vendor, and the correct response to it is not harder negotiation — it is a different shortlist.

## What each one is actually built around

Sprinklr Service is positioned as a full CCaaS replacement, not a help desk. Thirty-plus voice and digital channels feed one agent workspace and one reporting layer, with AI agents taking tier-one queries autonomously and Agent Copilot supplying suggestions and auto-summaries on everything else. Its real argument is unification across products: customer service, social listening, marketing, advertising, and engagement on one platform and one data model. For a global brand where the same complaint arrives by phone, surfaces on social, and informs a campaign, that is hard to replicate by wiring four best-of-breed tools together.

Kustomer is a service CRM organized around the customer rather than the ticket. Every purchase, interaction, and issue lands on one permanent timeline, so an agent sees the whole relationship without switching systems, across chat, email, voice, SMS, Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp. Custom objects and API access let teams model complicated product and order structures — which is why its base skews toward retail, travel, and consumer goods brands like Turo, SKIMS, Everlane, and Sweetgreen.

Scope versus depth: Sprinklr covers more ground, Kustomer goes deeper on the individual customer record.

## AI pricing: metered versus contracted

Kustomer's model is explicit and slightly uncomfortable. Customer-facing AI costs $0.60 per engaged conversation — charged when the AI actually engages, not for everything that arrives — and rep-facing AI is $40 per user per month, both on top of the $139 seat. The arithmetic gets steep fast: eight seats plus rep AI plus 2,000 AI-engaged conversations a month is about $31,584 a year, more than double the platform-only floor. All-inclusive bundles at roughly $129–$179 per user exist precisely because consumption is hard to forecast. The uncomfortable part is directional — the pitch is deflection and the meter is deflection, so your best-performing month is your most expensive. Teams with seasonal spikes almost always do better on the bundle, because à la carte prices your worst month rather than your average.

Sprinklr does not meter AI per conversation, but it prices partly on data volume. The platform ingests and analyzes large volumes of social and digital conversation, and that ingestion is a cost input independent of agent count. Two organizations with identical headcounts can receive materially different quotes, and a contract that fits at signature can double at renewal because listening volume grew. Get the measurement basis in writing.

## The costs neither pitch deck leads with

Kustomer bills storage overages at $50 per GB — worth a moment, because the entire product design is a permanent unified timeline retaining every interaction, so data accumulation is the intended behavior and it is metered. HIPAA compliance is another $25 per user per month, taking a healthcare seat to $164 before any AI. Voice and SMS are pay-as-you-go, complex implementations carry professional services fees, and there is no monthly billing at any tier.

Sprinklr's equivalent is the services load: implementation at 15–30% of contract value plus $10,000–$20,000 in training, annual and usually multi-year contracts, and no route to test at small scale now that self-serve has closed. Its breadth produces real implementation complexity — Sprinklr's own case studies report 210% ROI and $3M+ in benefits over three years precisely because those deployments are large.

## Who should pick what

- **Global enterprises needing customer service, social listening, and marketing on one data model** → Sprinklr Service. Few vendors do this convincingly.
- **Consumer brands with high conversation volume and complex order data** → Kustomer, for the unified timeline and custom objects.
- **500-plus seat contact centers spanning voice and a dozen digital channels** → Sprinklr, where channel breadth earns the contract.
- **Mid-market teams of 20 to 100 agents** → Kustomer, if you clear the eight-seat floor and can commit annually. Sprinklr is not available to you.
- **Healthcare support teams** → price Kustomer's $25 HIPAA add-on from the first call; it changes the comparison against vendors bundling compliance.
- **Teams with seasonal volume spikes** → Kustomer's all-inclusive bundle rather than à la carte AI.
- **Existing Sprinklr self-serve customers** → your access ended 30 April 2026; if you have not migrated or signed enterprise terms, that decision is overdue.

## Bottom line

These two stopped competing for the same buyer this year. Sprinklr Service is now purely an enterprise platform whose price is justified by unification across service, social, and marketing — and indefensible for anyone who just needs to answer tickets well. Kustomer sits a full tier below it commercially, with a legible $139 seat, a real eight-seat floor, and an AI meter that hands you a bigger invoice every time deflection works. Before comparing them, check whether you are even in Sprinklr's market: if a six-figure year-one commitment is out of scope, run Kustomer against Zendesk, Freshdesk, and Intercom instead, and treat any Sprinklr per-seat figure you encounter as historical.
