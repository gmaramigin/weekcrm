## TL;DR

- **Teamgate** gives you built-in analytics, forecasting, in-app Twilio dialing, and a named account manager starting at $31.92/user/month annually — plus a genuinely free two-user plan capped at 500 contacts.
- **Salesflare** captures contacts, meetings, email threads, and attachments out of your mailbox automatically, from $29/user/month annually, with a 30-day trial instead of a free tier.
- Teamgate's open API sits behind the $47.92 Growth tier. Salesflare's workflows and permissions sit behind the $49 Pro tier. Both entry plans are cheaper than the plan you will actually buy.

## The problem each one is solving

These CRMs answer different complaints. Teamgate's pitch is pipeline clarity: customizable stages, sales dashboards, revenue forecasting, and lead scoring, all without bolting on a BI tool. It assumes your reps will log their work and you need somewhere good to read it.

Salesflare assumes the opposite — that reps will not log anything, and that every CRM you have bought before died of neglect. Its automatic capture pulls people, companies, and threads from Gmail, Outlook, and LinkedIn so records stay current without typing. If your last rollout failed on adoption rather than features, that difference matters more than any spec sheet line.

That assumption comes with a hard prerequisite. Salesflare's capture only works against Gmail and Outlook mailboxes. On any other mail system you lose the exact feature you are paying for, and the comparison collapses before it starts.

## Pricing

Teamgate: Starter is free for two users, one pipeline, 500 contacts. Team runs $39.90/user/month monthly or $31.92 annually. Growth is $59.90 monthly, $47.92 annually.

Salesflare: Growth is $39/user/month monthly or $29 annually. Pro is $64 monthly, $49 annually. Enterprise is $99 annually with a five-seat minimum, so its true entry point is $495 a month.

Two structural quirks are worth pricing out. Teamgate's free plan ends abruptly — a third user takes a team from $0 to roughly $1,150 a year, with no cheap middle tier to land on. And Teamgate puts open API access plus Zapier in Growth, which means almost any team that wants the CRM talking to its marketing or billing stack pays $47.92, not $31.92.

Salesflare has no free plan at all, but its 30-day no-card trial is long enough to test properly. Its monthly penalty is unusually steep — 25 to 34% above the annual rate — so the honest comparison is annual-to-annual: $29 or $49 for Salesflare against $31.92 or $47.92 for Teamgate. At the tiers most teams land on, the two are within a few dollars of each other.

## Reporting versus enrichment

Teamgate bundles forecasting and sales dashboards into Team, its lower paid tier, with deeper analytics and lead scoring reserved for Growth. That is a lot of measurement machinery at a mid-market price.

Salesflare's reporting is competent but shallower — advanced forecasting takes workarounds. Where it spends its budget instead is on the front of the funnel: a built-in Lead Finder metered in credits, five a month on Growth and 100 on Pro. Five credits is a demonstration, not an allowance, which is the practical reason most prospecting teams end up on Pro. Salesflare also surfaces relationship strength across an account and flags connections going cold, which Teamgate does not attempt.

## Multi-currency, integrations, and other tier traps

International teams should read Teamgate's tier list carefully. Multi-currency is a Growth feature, so a European team quoting in euros and pounds cannot use Team at any headcount. Combined with the API paywall, Growth is the realistic Teamgate price for most companies, not the advertised entry rate.

Salesflare's equivalent trap is Pro. Workflows and user permissions live there, and once a team passes roughly four people, role-based visibility stops being optional. Neither vendor is hiding this, but both entry prices are optimistic.

## Support and hand-holding

Teamgate includes a dedicated account manager and 24/7 support on Team, which is unusual below $40 a seat and genuinely useful for a company with nobody who owns CRM administration internally.

Salesflare puts its equivalent behind Enterprise at $99 with the five-user minimum — full onboarding, custom training, and a named account manager. Below that, setup is self-service and takes minutes, which is fine for a small team and thin for a messy migration off an incumbent system.

## Who should pick what

- **Sales managers who want forecasting and dashboards built in** → Teamgate Team.
- **B2B teams whose last CRM failed because nobody updated it** → Salesflare, provided you are on Gmail or Outlook.
- **Two-person consultancies with a small warm network** → Teamgate's free Starter, until you cross 500 contacts.
- **Teams doing real outbound prospecting** → Salesflare Pro, for the 100 lead credits and workflows.
- **Companies quoting in several currencies or needing API access** → Teamgate Growth at $47.92, and budget for it from day one.
- **Anyone on a non-Google, non-Microsoft mail stack** → Teamgate, since Salesflare's core advantage does not apply.

## Bottom line

At the tiers real teams buy, these two cost about the same, so price is not the deciding factor. Teamgate is the better instrument panel: forecasting, analytics, and a human at the vendor who knows your setup. Salesflare is the better data-entry solution, and if adoption is where your CRM history goes to die, that is the more valuable problem to solve. Run Salesflare's 30-day trial against your live mailbox before deciding — if the automatic capture works on your setup, it will be obvious inside a week.
