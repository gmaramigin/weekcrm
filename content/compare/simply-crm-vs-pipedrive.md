## TL;DR

- Breadth versus execution. Simply CRM Professional at $22/user gives you quotes, invoices, sales orders, purchase orders, price books, projects, and a ticketing helpdesk. Pipedrive Growth at $39/seat gives you the best small-team pipeline in the category and nothing else.
- Simply CRM's entry tier is a decoy. Starter at $15 has no automation, no email integration, no invoicing, no reporting. Pipedrive's entry tier is a decoy for the same reason — Lite at $14 lacks full email sync and workflow automation.
- The real comparison is $22 against $39, and it is not a value comparison. It is a question of whether you need one system for the whole business or one excellent system for selling.

## What each product is actually competing with

Pipedrive competes with HubSpot Sales Hub and Zoho CRM. Its whole design is activity-based selling: a drag-and-drop pipeline, deals moving through stages, activities logged against records, and an interface most reps can operate on their first morning. Over 300 native integrations plus Zapier and Make cover everything it deliberately does not do.

Simply CRM is not really in that fight. The fair comparison for its Professional tier is Zoho One or a small Odoo deployment — suites that run selling, billing, delivery, and support from one database. Against those, $22 per user is a considered price rather than a bargain. Against Pipedrive, it looks cheap only because you are comparing different amounts of product.

## Pricing

Simply CRM lists $15 (Starter), $22 (Professional), and $41 (Enterprise) per user per month, all billed annually with no published monthly option. A five-person Professional deployment is a $1,320 up-front commitment, which is why the 30-day no-card trial matters.

Pipedrive restructured in late 2025 — Lite $14, Growth $39, Premium $64, Ultimate $79 per seat annually, replacing the Essential/Advanced/Professional/Power/Enterprise names still quoted on stale comparison sites. Monthly billing is punishing: Lite jumps to $24, Growth to $49. Ten seats on Growth is $4,680 a year annually, $5,880 monthly.

Both hide their real starting price one tier up. Neither has a free plan.

## The upgrade gate tells you what each vendor values

Simply CRM's expensive jump is Enterprise at $41 — 86% over Professional — and the thing that forces it is API access. Not team size, not volume. If anything in your stack talks to the CRM programmatically, you are on Enterprise whether you are three people or thirty. Enterprise also bundles AI prediction, custom layouts, ERP integration, and VOIP with no à la carte option. Map your integration needs before shortlisting it; that single decision moves the price by 86%.

Pipedrive's gate is cleaner. Growth to Premium is $25 a seat, and Premium bundles LeadBooster and Projects, which are separately billed below it. Buy both anyway and Premium is arithmetically cheaper; buy neither and stay on Growth. That is a calculation, not a hostage situation.

## Depth in each module

Simply CRM's weakness is stated plainly by the vendor: module depth is limited against dedicated tools, and power users hit ceilings. Complex deal structures, advanced forecasting, and territory management are not strengths. Its pipeline is competent — Kanban boards, dashboards, contacts and opportunities — and stops there.

Pipedrive's pipeline is the product. Two-way Gmail and Outlook sync, AI email assistance on higher tiers, a Sales Assistant on all paid plans surfacing stalled deals, and automation covering lead assignment, notifications, and field updates from Growth upward. Its weaknesses are a specialist's: thin reporting on lower tiers, no post-sale motion, no account hierarchies.

If your sales process is simple — a handful of stages, a small team, deals that close and turn into work — Simply CRM's pipeline is sufficient and the rest of the suite is the payoff. If selling is difficult and you have reps whose whole day is the CRM, Pipedrive earns the extra $17.

## Ecosystem and risk

Pipedrive is a large vendor with 500+ integrations and an enormous body of documentation, consultants, and third-party tooling. Onboarding takes days. Simply CRM has limited brand recognition, a smaller ecosystem, and fewer native integrations, though it offers 24/7 multilingual support, which is genuinely unusual at $22. Storage limits are unpublished at every tier — worth getting in writing before migrating a document-heavy business.

## Who should pick what

- **B2B sales teams of 2 to 50 reps** → Pipedrive Growth. This is what it is built for and there is no close second at the price.
- **Small agencies or distributors juggling a CRM, an invoicing tool, and a support inbox** → Simply CRM Professional. Replacing three subscriptions with one at $22 is the honest case for it.
- **Solo consultants tracking a couple dozen deals** → Pipedrive Lite at $14, and skip the suite entirely.
- **Teams with any custom integration requirement** → Pipedrive, or budget $41 for Simply CRM Enterprise from the start.
- **Businesses needing ticketing and project delivery alongside sales** → Simply CRM, since Pipedrive's Projects module only appears at Premium and its helpdesk does not exist.

## Bottom line

Simply CRM sells a lot of surface area for $22 and asks you to accept that none of it is best-in-class. That is a reasonable trade for a ten-person service business currently paying for three tools and reconciling between them — take the full 30-day trial and load real data, because a month is genuinely the minimum needed to test invoicing, projects, and a helpdesk together. Pipedrive sells one thing done well, charges $39 for the version people actually mean when they recommend it, and is transparent about everything it refuses to build. If revenue comes from working a pipeline, that focus is worth more than the extra modules. If revenue comes from delivering work you already sold, it is not.
