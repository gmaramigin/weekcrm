## TL;DR

- HubSpot's free CRM beats Teamgate's free plan by a wide margin: up to a million contacts against 500, and no two-user squeeze on the CRM side.
- The paid comparison inverts. Teamgate Team is $31.92/user annually with email sync, workflow automation, forecasting, in-app dialing, and a dedicated account manager. HubSpot Professional — the tier where sequences, custom reports, forecasting, and playbooks live — is $90/seat plus $1,500 one-time onboarding.
- Both have a pricing cliff. Teamgate's is $0 to roughly $1,150 a year for a three-person team. HubSpot's is $20 to $90 a seat with nothing between.

## The free plans are not comparable

Teamgate Starter is free for two users with one pipeline, 500 contacts, and core deal, contact, task, and calendar management. There is no time limit. But 500 contacts is a few weeks of list-building for any B2B team doing outbound, so Starter functions as an indefinite trial for a two-person consultancy working a warm network — not a plan a startup grows on.

HubSpot's free CRM covers contact and deal management, forms, and basic ticketing with contact capacity that no small business will exhaust. Its constraint is functional rather than numerical: no sequences, no custom reporting, and HubSpot branding stays on your forms and emails. Plenty of teams run on it for a year.

If the free tier is the deciding factor, this comparison is already over.

## Pricing

Teamgate has two paid tiers and no cheap step. Team is $39.90/user monthly or $31.92 annually; Growth is $59.90 or $47.92. The 20% annual discount is a larger absolute gap than most vendors offer, which makes monthly billing genuinely expensive rather than a convenience premium.

HubSpot runs free, then Starter at $20/seat, Professional at $90/seat annually ($100 monthly) with a one-time $1,500 onboarding fee, then Enterprise at $150 with a $3,500 fee. Those charges are mandatory on self-serve and land on the first invoice. At five seats, $1,500 adds 28% to year one; at fifty it is a rounding error. HubSpot's fixed fees hit small teams hardest.

Ten people, one year: Teamgate Team is $3,830. HubSpot Professional is $12,300 in year one and $10,800 after. HubSpot Starter for the same team is $2,400 with no onboarding fee, but without sequences or custom reporting.

## What forces the upgrade

Teamgate's Growth trigger is the API. Open API access and Zapier both sit in Growth, so any team connecting the CRM to marketing tools, billing, or a warehouse pays $47.92 rather than $31.92 — a 50% uplift for a requirement nearly every sales team has. Multi-currency is Growth-only too, so a European team quoting in three currencies cannot use Team at any headcount.

HubSpot's trigger is the Starter-to-Professional cliff. Sequences, the custom report builder, forecasting, and playbooks are all Professional-and-up with no partial path. Need one and you pay 4.5×. One structural saving worth knowing: only people who *do* things need paid seats — managers who read dashboards sit on free view-only seats, and teams routinely over-buy here.

## Support and who administers the thing

Teamgate includes a dedicated account manager on Team at under $40 a seat, plus 24/7 support, with phone and video support at Growth. A named contact at the vendor on a sub-$40 plan is unusual — most reserve that for enterprise agreements — and for a small team with nobody owning CRM administration it is worth real money.

HubSpot's equivalent is scale rather than intimacy: HubSpot Academy, a 1,500+ app marketplace, and more documentation than any competitor. You will find an answer, but you will find it yourself or pay a solutions partner. Partners will sometimes absorb or discount the $1,500 onboarding fee on a meaningful annual contract — worth pushing on.

## Scope and where the bill goes sideways

Teamgate does sales. Pipeline stages, analytics and forecasting without a BI add-on, Twilio-powered calling and SMS from contact records, lead scoring at Growth. Its ecosystem is narrower than HubSpot's and it does not attempt marketing or service. That focus keeps the bill predictable: seats times rate.

HubSpot's Marketing Hub does not bill on seats at all — it bills on marketing contacts, meaning everyone you can email. A webinar, an imported trade-show list, a successful campaign can push you across a threshold, and the upgrade is automatic. Nobody approves it; you find it on the invoice. Model it as a separate calculation from seats. Also budget for renewal: promotional Starter rates as low as $7 a seat revert toward $20, and there is no mid-term downgrade.

## Who should pick what

- **Marketing-led companies running inbound campaigns** → HubSpot. The unified data model across CRM, email, and site is the whole argument and it is a good one.
- **Small sales-only teams of 3 to 30** → Teamgate Team at $31.92. Paying HubSpot Professional prices for a marketing platform nobody opens is the classic expensive mistake.
- **International teams quoting in multiple currencies** → Teamgate Growth at $47.92, or HubSpot. Teamgate's Team tier is off the table regardless of size.
- **Anyone needing API access on a budget** → HubSpot Starter at $20 is worth comparing against Teamgate Growth at $47.92, since Teamgate gates integration to its top tier.
- **Bootstrapped startups expecting to grow** → HubSpot's free CRM. Teamgate's jump from $0 to about $1,150 a year at the third hire is abrupt, and HubSpot's free ceiling is far higher.
- **Database-heavy businesses with large, low-engagement lists** → not HubSpot Marketing Hub. Contact-tier pricing charges you for people who will never buy.

## Bottom line

These products compete for maybe one buyer in five. Teamgate makes sense for a self-contained sales team that wants forecasting, automation, dialing, and a human at the vendor who knows their setup, at roughly a third of HubSpot Professional's price — provided the integration and multi-currency requirements are listed honestly before choosing between its two tiers. HubSpot makes sense when marketing and sales genuinely share a database and the campaign engine earns its keep. Start with the free tiers on both if you can, but recognize that Teamgate's is a two-person courtesy and HubSpot's is a real operating plan.
