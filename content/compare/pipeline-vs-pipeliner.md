## First, the names

These are two separate companies. One letter apart, no relationship, and both have changed names at least once, which makes searching for either genuinely confusing.

**Pipeline CRM** lives at pipelinecrm.com and used to be called PipelineDeals. Small and mid-sized US sales teams, three tiers from $25 to $49 per user, free US-based phone support on every plan.

**Pipeliner CRM** lives at pipelinersales.com and now trades as **Coevera** — the pricing pages redirect there. Mid-market visual sales management, four tiers from $65 to $150 per user, annual billing only.

If a comparison site quoted you a price, check which domain it came from. Stale figures from both rebrands are everywhere, and the products differ by roughly 3× at the tier most teams land on.

## TL;DR

- Pipeline CRM sells simplicity at a fair price. Pipeliner sells visual depth and reporting at a mid-market price, and unbundles aggressively.
- The realistic entry costs are $33/user (Pipeline CRM Develop) and $2,340/year (Pipeliner Starter at its three-seat annual floor). That gap decides most evaluations before features enter the conversation.
- Both gate the thing you probably want. Pipeline CRM puts email automation behind Grow at $49. Pipeliner puts workflow automation behind Enterprise at $115 and sells its AI assistant separately.

## Pricing

Pipeline CRM: Start $25, Develop $33, Grow $49 per user annually ($29/$39/$59 monthly), Enterprise quoted. Capacity separates the tiers, not features — 1 pipeline and 250 active deals on Start, 2 and 2,500 on Develop, 5 and unlimited on Grow. That deal cap is account-wide, so five reps with fifty open opportunities each have already filled Start. Treat it as a solo plan.

Pipeliner: Starter $65 with a three-user minimum, Business $85, Enterprise $115, Unlimited $150 — all annual, no self-serve monthly option. The smallest possible commitment is three seats for twelve months.

At ten users on the tier each vendor recommends, Pipeline CRM Develop is $330/month and Pipeliner Business is $850/month. That is the honest comparison, and it is not close.

## Where the money goes after the license

Both vendors put items outside the tier price, but they unbundle different things.

Pipeline CRM's add-ons are narrow and optional: data enrichment $19–$69/month, email validation $25 flat for 5,000 requests, and Pipeline Connect at $99–$249/month for managed integrations. Validation is cheap insurance for sending reputation. Pipeline Connect is the one to push back on — with anyone on staff who can operate Zapier, the same connections cost a fraction.

Pipeliner unbundles more aggressively, and the separated items are not peripheral: Voyager II (the AI assistant that features throughout its marketing), Account Health scoring, quote version management, approval processes, scheduled reports, custom entities, the business card scanner, geolocation. A team wanting the product as demonstrated should assume an effective per-seat cost above the list rate and negotiate one bundled quote.

## Visual depth versus getting out of the way

Pipeliner's differentiator is genuinely visual. Kanban, list, bubble chart, and archive views over the same data; relationship maps and account hierarchy charts for complex B2B accounts; document management with role-based access and version tracking; offline-capable mobile apps for field reps. Voyager AI handles forecasting and anomaly detection. Some users find the interface overwhelming until configured, the natural cost of that much visual surface.

Pipeline CRM does not compete on any of that. Deal and lead tracking, native email drip campaigns, task automation, dashboards, and reporting that is solid without being granular. Its interface is functional rather than polished and its ecosystem is smaller than Pipedrive's or HubSpot's.

The question worth answering honestly: does your sales leadership run the business off CRM reports, or off spreadsheets built from CRM exports? If the second, Pipeliner's depth is not being consumed and you are paying triple for it.

## Automation gates, compared

Pipeline CRM's gate is email sequencing. Start and Develop do not include it; Grow at $49 does, 96% above the headline $25. Teams building an outbound motion should price Grow from the start.

Pipeliner's gate is Automatizer, its workflow engine, at Enterprise ($115) with 25 processes and 100 at Unlimited ($150). If automation is central to why you are shortlisting Pipeliner, your price is $115 per seat and the $65 headline is irrelevant.

Both vendors put their most-wanted capability two tiers up. Budget accordingly.

## Administration and support

Pipeline CRM includes US-based phone support on every plan, unusual at $25 and a real differentiator for teams without internal CRM expertise. Setup is quick because there is little to configure. Pipeliner's argument against Salesforce is administration: no-code configuration a sales leader can change without a certified admin, which removes an ongoing cost that never appears on any price list. The rebrand to Coevera has left documentation and support resources mid-transition, though — worth asking about directly.

## Who should pick what

- **Small US sales teams in construction, manufacturing, or distribution** → Pipeline CRM Develop at $33. Phone support included, no ceremony.
- **Solo operators or two-person teams** → Pipeline CRM. Pipeliner's three-seat annual floor makes it a non-starter at that size.
- **Mid-market teams of 5 to 50 wanting Salesforce-grade visibility without an implementation project** → Pipeliner Business at $85, or Enterprise if you need Automatizer.
- **Anyone selling into complex accounts with multiple stakeholders** → Pipeliner. The relationship maps and hierarchy charts are its clearest advantage.
- **Teams running multiple distinct sales processes** → count them first. Pipeline CRM allows 1, 2, and 5 pipelines by tier; new business plus renewals plus a partner channel means Grow.

## Bottom line

The name collision is the only thing these two share. Pipeline CRM is a well-priced deal tracker for teams that want a CRM and not a platform — count your open opportunities before assuming $25 applies, because the 250-deal account cap arrives faster than anyone expects. Pipeliner, or Coevera, is a mid-market visual sales system priced near Salesforce Sales Cloud and justified by needing far less implementation. It earns the premium only if your reps genuinely work in those visual views and your managers genuinely run forecasts from them. Use both 14-day trials against real pipeline data, since neither vendor offers a cheap monthly escape once the annual term starts.
