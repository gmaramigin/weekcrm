## TL;DR

- **Membrain** sells by sales motion, not team size: Prospecting $49, Active Pipeline $69, Account Growth $89, Elevate $89 per coach — plus flat account-level upgrades (Insight Engine from $199/mo, Automation+ $299, API+ $499).
- **Pipedrive** sells four seat tiers: Lite $14, Growth $39, Premium $64, Ultimate $79, all annual. Growth is the realistic team entry point; Lite has no email sync and no workflow automation.
- The real question is not budget. It is whether you have a sales process you want the software to enforce, or a pipeline you want it to display.

## One tool has an opinion about how you sell

Pipedrive is deliberately neutral. Its center of gravity is a drag-and-drop Kanban pipeline built around activity-based selling — log the calls, move the cards, and the tool stays out of your judgment. Most reps are productive on day one without training, which is why it wins so many migrations off spreadsheets.

Membrain takes the opposite position: how you sell matters as much as what you sell. Its stage-by-stage process playbooks define qualification criteria and required evidence at each milestone, and Elevate adds coaching workflows, skill assessments, and manager enablement on top. If your organization has committed to a methodology and cannot get reps to follow it consistently, nothing mainstream in this category does that job as directly.

The flip side is rigidity. Without a defined process to encode, Membrain's machinery is overhead you pay for and route around, and Pipedrive's flexibility becomes the feature rather than the compromise.

## Pricing works on completely different axes

Pipedrive multiplies seats by a tier and stops. No contact caps, no record limits, no metered API. The bill you calculate in January is the bill you pay in November, with add-ons (LeadBooster, Projects on Lite and Growth) the only variable, and those only move when you decide to buy them.

Membrain's modules stack per user. A rep who prospects, closes, and grows accounts carries three licenses — $207 a month before extensions, past Salesforce Enterprise territory. That is not automatically a problem, but it demands discipline: license SDRs on Prospecting, AEs on Active Pipeline, account managers on Account Growth, managers on Elevate, and an eight-person commercial team can average nearer $70. Hand everyone everything and Membrain becomes one of the most expensive CRMs you can buy.

Then there are Membrain's platform upgrades, charged flat per account rather than per seat. API+ at $499 a month lands on a five-person Active Pipeline deployment costing $345 and more than doubles the bill. Price that line before anything else if your team is small and integration is non-negotiable.

## Where each one's cheap tier disappoints

Pipedrive's $14 Lite is a marketing number. It has no full email sync and no workflow automation — the two things sales teams actually buy a CRM for. It is a fair price for a solo consultant tracking twenty deals and a false economy for a team, which typically upgrades to Growth within a quarter. Note also that Pipedrive rebuilt its lineup in late 2025; any source still quoting Essential, Advanced, Professional, Power, and Enterprise is describing plans that no longer exist.

Membrain's $49 Prospecting module is not a cheap version of the product either — it is a different product, for structured outbound. The conventional CRM experience lives in Active Pipeline at $69. Neither vendor's headline price is what a working team pays.

## Evaluation and commercial terms

Pipedrive runs a 14-day trial with no card, no seat minimum, and sells from a single seat on every plan. You can be live in days and decide for yourself.

Membrain publishes no free trial, no annual discount, no minimum, and no contract length — the site defers to your agreement, so evaluation means talking to sales. That removes a public anchor for budgeting, but it also means the rate card is a starting point. Ask directly about annual commitment discounts and about bundling the platform upgrades, since the $499 API+ line is the least defensible charge for a small buyer.

One more Membrain quirk: prices are quoted at numeric parity across USD, EUR, and SEK. Depending on the exchange rate, €49 is not $49, and European buyers should do the arithmetic rather than assume equivalence.

## Complex deals versus fast deals

Membrain is aimed at manufacturing, professional services, MSPs, and enterprise B2B — multi-stakeholder deals with long cycles where Account Growth's relationship mapping and whitespace analysis earn their keep. Its forecasting comes from structured deal scoring rather than a rep's gut feel.

Pipedrive is aimed at the two-to-fifty-rep sales team living in deal stages, with 300+ native integrations plus Zapier and Make, and Sales Assistant surfacing stalled deals across all paid plans. It is explicitly weak at complex account hierarchies and post-sale customer success workflows — the exact terrain Membrain is built for.

Watch Membrain's Insight Engine credits, too: they reset monthly with no rollover, which stings in slower months.

## Who should pick what

- **Organizations running MEDDIC, Challenger, or a bespoke methodology they want enforced** → Membrain Active Pipeline, licensed by role.
- **SMB sales teams that want a pipeline and low friction** → Pipedrive Growth at $39.
- **Solo consultants and two-person shops** → Pipedrive Lite, understanding what it omits.
- **Expansion-led businesses doing key account planning** → Membrain Account Growth.
- **Small teams needing deep API integration on a tight budget** → Pipedrive; Membrain's $499 API+ fee is punishing below about twenty seats.
- **Teams whose add-on list includes chat capture and post-deal projects** → Pipedrive Premium, which bundles what Growth bills separately.

## Bottom line

Pipedrive is the safer default and the easier sell internally: predictable per-seat cost, a two-week trial, no minimums, and adoption that rarely fails. Membrain is the better answer for a narrow, real buyer — a consultative B2B organization with a methodology worth enforcing and enough discipline to license each role for the motion it actually runs. Model the stacking math and the account-level upgrade fees before you fall for the coaching demo, and if you cannot name the process you want enforced, buy Pipedrive.
