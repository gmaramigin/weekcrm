## TL;DR

- **Lone Wolf Relationships** is the product LionDesk customers were moved onto when that CRM closed in September 2025. Video email and text drips are its inheritance; a published price is not.
- **Wise Agent** charges one flat account rate for up to five team members, includes transaction management and 24/7 human support, and has held Forbes Advisor's Best Real Estate CRM slot three years running.
- Wise Agent rates 4.5 here against 3.9 for Relationships, and the pricing transparency gap explains a good chunk of that.
- Pick Relationships if you are already inside Lone Wolf's stack. Otherwise Wise Agent is the safer default.

## One product inherited a customer base, the other earned one

Relationships did not grow into its user list. LionDesk shut down and its customers were migrated, some of them reporting features that did not survive the move. That history colors the product: it is the lowest-friction landing spot for ex-LionDesk agents, and its roadmap answers to Lone Wolf's broader consolidation rather than to this product's own users.

Wise Agent arrived the slow way. It has been serving solo agents at a flat price long enough to build a content library, a transaction module, and a support desk staffed around the clock. Nothing about it is fashionable. Most of it works.

## Price transparency is the real dividing line

Wise Agent posts its numbers. The CRM plan is $49 monthly, $42 on annual billing at $499 a year, covering up to five team members. Adding WiseSocial takes it to $69 monthly or $59 annual. Add-ons are small and itemized: $20 for another five-member pack, $20 per additional individual login, $5 for unlimited lead enhancement credits, $11 plus an $80 carrier registration for WiseText SMS. Teams above roughly fifteen agents move to a quoted Enterprise plan. No contracts, no setup fee.

Lone Wolf publishes nothing. Third parties report an entry point around $25 a month, though $33.25 also circulates — and when two reported starting prices disagree, neither is a rate card. A Professional plan is cited at $249. That is a tenfold step with no visible middle, which usually signals two different buyer segments rather than a feature ladder. Brokerage and franchise deals are custom-quoted with negotiated volume discounts, so two similar firms can land on different numbers. EZ Texting appears as an add-on with no published cost at all.

The practical consequence: you can budget Wise Agent from a webpage and you cannot budget Relationships without a sales call. If Relationships genuinely lands at $25, it undercuts Wise Agent for a lone agent. If your configuration lands nearer Professional, you are paying roughly five times Wise Agent's rate for a lighter product.

## Feature depth

Wise Agent covers more ground. Contract-to-close transaction management with checklists and document storage is built in, which most CRMs at this price make you buy separately. Landing pages, drip campaigns, a stock library of postcards and email sequences, and reporting all sit inside the base plan. WiseSocial handles automated listing posts and branded listing videos for the extra $17.

Relationships keeps a narrower profile: contact management, video email, text marketing, and straightforward drips. The video email is a real differentiator carried over from LionDesk and worth something to agents who use it. Its automation is lighter than Realvolve or Follow Up Boss, and heavy users hit the ceiling.

Neither generates leads. No IDX website, no managed ad stack, on either side. Whatever you spend filling these CRMs will dwarf the subscription, which is an argument for not overthinking the software decision and a stronger argument for not overpaying.

## Support and access model

Wise Agent includes 24/7 phone and chat support at the base tier, which almost nothing else in this price bracket does. The catch to know is logins: the five team members share access by default, and separate individual logins are $20 each. A genuine five-agent team wanting five logins pays around $122 a month, not $42 — still cheap, but not the headline.

Relationships is simple to onboard, usable the same afternoon you sign up, with a free trial promoted on the product page. Given the pricing opacity, that trial is the only honest way to evaluate it before entering a sales conversation.

## Who should pick what

- **Ex-LionDesk agents who do not want to re-platform** → Relationships, purely for continuity.
- **Agents already running Lone Wolf transactions or back office** → Relationships, for one account and one vendor.
- **Solo agents shopping fresh with no ecosystem tie** → Wise Agent.
- **Small teams of two to five sharing a workspace** → Wise Agent, decisively; the flat rate has no equivalent.
- **Anyone who needs contract-to-close tracking in the CRM** → Wise Agent.
- **Buyers who refuse to negotiate for a base price** → Wise Agent, since Relationships will not give you one.

## Bottom line

Wise Agent is the stronger product and the more honest purchase. It costs a known amount, includes transaction management and real support, and its flat account pricing is close to unique in a per-seat category. Lone Wolf Relationships is worth a look only if you inherited it from LionDesk or already buy other Lone Wolf products — and even then, start the free trial before the sales call, and ask what triggers the jump to Professional. A tenfold price step is not something to find out about later.
