## TL;DR

- **SupportBee**: $17/seat/month on Startup, $21 on Enterprise (annual); $20 and $25 monthly. Unlimited email inboxes, unlimited tickets, no metering of anything except seats. No live chat, no AI. Rated 3.9.
- **Groove**: $12 / $20 / $35 per user per month, annual billing only. Shared inboxes capped at 2 / 5 / 25, Lite User allowances of 0 / 10 / 50, AI Draft credits metered at $0.25. Rated 4.3, with 130,000+ support professionals claimed.
- Groove's entry price is lower and its ceiling is much higher. SupportBee's price barely moves and neither does its feature set — which for some teams is exactly the appeal.

## Inbox count is where these two fundamentally disagree

SupportBee includes unlimited email inboxes on both tiers. Groove treats inboxes as the primary meter: two on Standard, five on Plus, twenty-five on Pro.

That single difference drives most real-world cost comparisons. A company running support@, billing@, partners@ and a regional address needs four inboxes. On SupportBee that is included at $17. On Groove it pushes you from Standard's $12 to Plus's $20 — and Plus is where SLA management, business hours and the AI assist features live, so the upgrade brings capability along with the cost.

Run the arithmetic on your own address list before comparing headline rates. Teams with one queue find Groove cheaper; teams with five or six often find SupportBee is.

## The full ladder, side by side

At ten seats on annual billing: SupportBee Startup is $2,040 a year, SupportBee Enterprise $2,520, Groove Standard $1,440, Groove Plus $2,400, Groove Pro $4,200.

Two structural notes complicate that table. Groove has no genuine month-to-month option at the advertised rates, so five agents on Standard is $720 committed up front rather than $60 you can stop paying; SupportBee does offer monthly, at an 18–19% premium.

And SupportBee's tier decision is not about scale at all. Startup allows one knowledge base and one team, and puts business hours and the customer portal in Enterprise. A four-person team supporting two brands with a response-time commitment is on $21 from day one. Business hours in particular is the sleeper requirement — without it your SLA clock runs against wall time and a Friday-evening ticket accrues breach all weekend.

## What Groove has and SupportBee simply does not

Live chat, for a start, plus social messaging alongside email in the same queue. Then rule-based routing, auto-tagging, escalation workflows, collision detection, configurable SLA targets with alerts, and native integrations to Slack, Salesforce, HubSpot and Stripe.

The AI split is worth understanding precisely. Summarization, sentiment analysis, auto-tagging and writing assist are bundled from Plus upward. AI Drafts — where the system composes the reply — are metered at $0.25 per credit. A five-agent Plus team paying $100 in seats that generates 400 drafts adds another $100, taking the effective rate to $40 per seat, above Pro's list price.

Groove also has Lite Users: people who read and comment on threads without working the queue. Ten free on Plus, fifty on Pro, none on Standard. Where engineers and account managers get looped into support conversations constantly, that allowance is worth more than the $8 tier difference implies. SupportBee has no cheap seat class at all.

## When less product is the right product

SupportBee's case is narrow and legitimate. The interface looks and behaves like email, so a new hire is productive immediately. Snippets handle repeat replies without a macro engine to configure. Unlimited tickets and inboxes mean the bill is seats times a number and nothing else — no conversation caps, no AI meter, no channel add-ons. KBee provides a branded self-service help center without a separate subscription.

The missing AI matters less than it sounds for one profile: technical B2B support, professional services, complex products where nearly every ticket is bespoke. Deflection rates are low there, and an AI layer adds cost without removing work. That is precisely SupportBee's market. Everyone else should notice that competitors in the same price band offer automation it does not.

## Portability and commitment

One flag worth resolving before signing with Groove: data exports are a Pro-only feature at $35 per user. Gating portability behind the top tier is a real lock-in mechanic on a tool whose value is your accumulated conversation history. If you plan to sit on Standard or Plus, get in writing what you can retrieve and in what format. SupportBee's monthly option, by contrast, means you can leave on thirty days' notice.

## Who should pick what

- **Team with one or two queues wanting the cheapest real shared inbox** → Groove Standard at $12.
- **Team running four or more support addresses** → SupportBee, where inboxes are unlimited at $17.
- **Support that needs live chat or social channels** → Groove. SupportBee is email only, by design.
- **B2B or technical support where every ticket is bespoke** → SupportBee. The absent AI costs you nothing you would have used.
- **Company with engineers and AMs who need read access** → Groove Plus, for the ten free Lite Users.
- **Anyone with a contractual response-time commitment** → SupportBee Enterprise at $21 (business hours) or Groove Plus at $20 (SLA management). Not the cheapest tier of either.
- **Teams that need to trial alongside an existing tool** → SupportBee, since Groove's rates require an annual commitment.

## Bottom line

Groove is the more capable product and the better-rated one, and at $12 a seat with two inboxes it is genuinely good value for a team with a single queue. It gets expensive in two specific ways: inbox count pushes you up the ladder faster than headcount does, and AI Drafts are metered where you will use them most. SupportBee is deliberately smaller — email, snippets, a knowledge base, unlimited inboxes, a flat bill — and it does not pretend to be growing into anything. Below roughly fifteen agents on an email-only workflow with several addresses, that restraint is worth paying for. Above it, or the moment chat matters, Groove is the one that keeps working.
