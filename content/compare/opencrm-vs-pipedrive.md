## TL;DR

- **OpenCRM** charges £44 per user per month, excluding VAT, with no tiers and no feature gates. That covers sales, helpdesk, projects, invoicing, inventory, email marketing, eSignature, 2TB of storage, and unlimited custom fields.
- **Pipedrive** runs $14 (Lite), $39 (Growth), $64 (Premium), $79 (Ultimate) per seat annually, with a 14-day trial and no free plan. Sales only — no marketing platform, no help desk, no CMS.
- UK buyers should budget £52.80 gross on OpenCRM. Pipedrive's monthly billing costs 26% more on Growth and 71% more on Lite.
- Neither is really competing for the other's core buyer, but they collide constantly on UK shortlists.

## A ladder versus no ladder

Pipedrive's entire design is the pipeline. Drag-and-drop deal stages, activity-based selling, two-way Gmail and Outlook sync, 500-plus integrations, a Sales Assistant that flags stalled deals. Reps who have never used a CRM are productive on day one, which is a genuine achievement and the reason it is recommended so often.

OpenCRM is a British business suite that happens to lead with CRM. Ticketing, project tracking, quotes and invoicing, inventory, event management, and marketing sit alongside the pipeline, all included, all at the same price for every user. Its published position is blunt: no upgrade ladder, no feature gates.

The philosophical difference matters more than any feature list. Pipedrive asks you to predict which capabilities you will need and buy the rung that holds them. OpenCRM removes the question.

## Where the tier ladder bites

Pipedrive's $14 entry price does heavy marketing work, and what it buys is narrower than most assume. Lite has no full email sync and no workflow automation — the two things sales teams actually buy a CRM to do. Solo consultants tracking twenty deals are fine there. Teams upgrade within a quarter, so treat $39 Growth as the real entry price.

Above that, Premium at $64 bundles LeadBooster and Projects, which are separately licensed on Lite and Growth. That makes the Growth-to-Premium step a calculation rather than a judgment call: if you would buy both add-ons anyway, Premium is cheaper. Ultimate at $79 is about permission complexity and rarely necessary below a few dozen reps.

One trap worth naming: Pipedrive rebuilt its lineup in late 2025, retiring the Essential, Advanced, Professional, Power, and Enterprise names. Comparison sites still quoting five tiers are describing a structure that no longer exists.

OpenCRM has no equivalent trap, and that is the point. The £44 you calculate on day one is the £44 you pay in month eleven. What you give up is the option to pay less for less — if a simple deal pipeline is genuinely all you need, £44 buys a lot of capability you will never open.

## Running the numbers

Ten seats on Pipedrive Growth is $4,680 a year on annual billing, $5,880 monthly. Ten seats on OpenCRM is £5,280 ex-VAT, £6,336 gross — roughly $6,700 at typical rates. So OpenCRM costs about 40% more than the Pipedrive tier most teams land on.

That gap closes fast if you would otherwise buy a help desk, an invoicing tool, and a project tracker separately, since those are three more subscriptions plus the integration work to connect them. It widens at scale: OpenCRM publishes no volume discount, so fifty users pay the same per head as five, while Pipedrive at least competes on published seat rates across a range. Above thirty users it is worth asking OpenCRM directly, because published flat pricing does not always mean fixed negotiated pricing.

## Residency, support, and ecosystem

For UK public sector, NHS-adjacent bodies, legal practices, and regulated financial firms, OpenCRM's UK data centers and UK-based support team remove a compliance argument that can stall a purchase for months. Same time zone, same jurisdiction, people who understand UK business practice.

Pipedrive's advantage runs the other way. Its integration marketplace is enormous, its ecosystem is global, and connecting it to a US-centric stack is trivial. OpenCRM's catalog is narrower and its integrations with American tools less developed, so anyone with an unusual connectivity requirement should check it explicitly rather than assuming "all features" covers it.

Evaluation paths differ too. Pipedrive gives you 14 days with no card. OpenCRM's evaluation runs through the vendor, so confirm what trial or demo period is available before committing to a paid month.

## Who should pick what

- **UK firms with data residency in their procurement rules** → OpenCRM, where the compliance conversation is already settled.
- **B2B sales teams of two to fifty living in deal stages** → Pipedrive Growth.
- **Businesses that would otherwise buy CRM, helpdesk, invoicing, and projects separately** → OpenCRM, where the bundle math works.
- **Solo consultants tracking a handful of deals** → Pipedrive Lite at $14; OpenCRM has no cheap entry point.
- **Teams outside the UK** → Pipedrive, since OpenCRM's residency pitch is worth nothing to you and sterling adds currency exposure.
- **Growing teams above fifty seats** → negotiate both; OpenCRM's flat rate loses ground to vendors with volume bands.

## Bottom line

Pipedrive is the better sales tool and the cheaper one for teams that only sell. It is fast to adopt, easy to budget, and unmatched at what it does — just do not buy Lite expecting a team CRM. OpenCRM is the better purchase for UK businesses that want functional breadth, onshore data, and complete certainty about the invoice, and it is one of very few vendors where the price on the website is the price forever. Budget the VAT-inclusive figure on one side and the annual commitment on the other, and the comparison gets honest quickly.
