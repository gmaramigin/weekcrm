## TL;DR

- **Missive** is a collaborative inbox: real-time co-authoring of drafts, internal comments on threads, and email, SMS, WhatsApp, live chat and social DMs in one view. $14/user/month Starter, $24 Productive, $36 Business — 20% off annually.
- **Help Scout** is a help desk that looks like email: shared inboxes, the Beacon widget, a Docs knowledge base, and an AI assistant that resolves an average of 70% of routine requests. Free for 5 users, $25/user Standard, $45 Plus, $75 Pro.
- Missive's pricing is gated by hard user caps; Help Scout's is gated by inbox counts and metered AI. Neither is dishonest, but both create step changes you should map before signing.
- Choose Missive if the problem is teammates colliding on replies. Choose Help Scout if the problem is ticket volume.

## Collaboration tool versus support tool

Missive's distinguishing trick is that two people can write the same draft at once, with private comments living alongside the thread and none of it visible to the customer. Conversations get assigned like tasks with statuses and due dates. It handles the full channel spread — email, SMS, WhatsApp, chat, social DMs — in one interface, and its AI features bill through your own AI provider account rather than a platform surcharge.

Help Scout goes the other direction: fewer collaboration mechanics, more support machinery. Beacon surfaces knowledge base articles proactively before a customer opens a chat, which suppresses ticket volume at the source. Docs gives you a public knowledge base. The AI assistant handles routine requests and hands off to a human when it can't. Native connectors to Salesforce, Jira, HubSpot, Shopify and 100+ others sit on higher tiers.

The honest framing: Missive is a communications layer, not a CRM, and its contact history and deal tracking are limited. Help Scout is a customer support platform and doesn't try to be an internal service desk or ITSM tool.

## Two very different pricing cliffs

Missive gates on headcount. Starter stops at 5 users, Productive at 50, Business is unlimited — and a tier change reprices every existing seat. Five people on Starter monthly is $70. Six people is $144. Adding one teammate more than doubles the bill, and nothing about that person's usage explains it. If you're sitting at four or five users, know that number before you hire.

A second ceiling: Productive caps automations at 1,000. Most teams build twenty rules and forget it exists. Agencies using Missive as a routing engine — a rule per client, per form, per SLA condition — hit it, and the only exit is Business at $36. Shared accounts are also derived from headcount at five per user pooled across the org, awkward for a four-person agency managing thirty client mailboxes.

Help Scout gates on inboxes and AI. Standard includes 2 inboxes, Plus 5, Pro 25, with extras at $10/month annual and Docs sites at $20/month each — multi-brand companies get nickel-and-dimed. AI Answers is metered at $0.75 per resolution outside the subscription entirely: a 12-seat Standard team pays $300 for seats, and a month where the AI closes 600 conversations adds $450 on top. Set a spending cap on day one.

Pro's 10-seat minimum is the other trap. A six-person team wanting Pro pays for ten — $750/month versus $270 on Plus. Below ten agents, buy extra inboxes on Plus instead; you'd need about thirty before the math flips.

## The free-tier difference

Help Scout has a permanent free plan for 5 users with 1 inbox, 1 Docs site, and 100 contacts a month. That last number makes it a demo allowance rather than a working plan, but it exists forever and gives you a zero-cost fallback.

Missive has no free tier at all per its current pricing — a 30-day trial with full features and no card required, and then you pay or lose access. For a team that might lose budget, that's a real structural difference against Help Scout, Hiver, or Zoho Desk.

## What they cost at three team sizes

Annual billing, seats only:

| Team | Missive | Help Scout |
|---|---|---|
| 3 people | $11.20/user (Starter) | $0 (Free) or $21/user (Standard) |
| 6 people | $19.20/user (Productive, forced) | $21/user (Standard) |
| 20 people | $19.20/user (Productive) | $21/user Standard, $37.80 Plus |

Missive is cheaper on paper at most sizes, and materially cheaper at 20 seats on Productive. The comparison shifts once you add Help Scout's AI Answers — which may pay for itself in deflected volume, or may not, depending entirely on how repetitive your queue is.

## Who should pick what

- **Agencies and ops teams where several people touch the same conversation** → Missive. Co-authoring and internal comments are the reason to buy it and nothing in Help Scout matches them.
- **SaaS or ecommerce support teams drowning in repetitive tickets** → Help Scout. Beacon plus Docs plus AI Answers attacks volume; Missive attacks coordination.
- **Teams hovering at exactly five users** → price Missive's next hire before you make it, or start on Help Scout Standard where growth is linear.
- **Multi-brand businesses supporting several products** → run Help Scout's inbox and Docs add-ons through a spreadsheet first, then compare against Front or Zendesk.
- **Teams needing WhatsApp and social DMs in the same queue as email** → Missive, clearly.
- **Anyone who needs a permanent zero-cost fallback** → Help Scout Free.

## Bottom line

Ask where the friction is. If replies go out late because nobody knows who's handling a thread, and two people occasionally answer the same customer, Missive fixes that directly and costs less per seat while doing it — just map the 5-user and 1,000-rule ceilings against your growth plan. If replies go out late because there are simply too many of them, Help Scout's deflection stack is the right tool, and the $0.75-per-resolution meter is fair as long as you cap it.

Both vendors revise caps and rates. Confirm against missiveapp.com/pricing and helpscout.com/pricing before committing to a year.
