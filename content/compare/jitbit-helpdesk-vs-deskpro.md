## TL;DR

- **Jitbit Helpdesk** charges by agent band, not per seat: $29, $69, $129, $249 a month in the cloud, or a one-time self-hosted licence at $2,199, $3,799, or $6,499 including one year of upgrades and support.
- **Deskpro** charges per agent with seat minimums that matter more than the rate: $39/agent (min 5), $59 (min 10), $99 (min 25). Deskpro Private, the on-premise edition, is quoted separately and not published.
- Jitbit is email ticketing done conventionally and cheaply. Deskpro is genuine omnichannel with deep automation, SLA enforcement, and included AI from the $59 tier.
- At twenty agents the gap is roughly $249 a month versus $1,180. Whether that gap is a bargain or a false economy depends entirely on how many channels you support.

## Different answers to the same compliance question

Both vendors exist on shortlists for the same reason: procurement said no cloud, or data has to stay in a specific jurisdiction. Almost everyone else in the category abandoned on-premise years ago.

Jitbit's answer is a published perpetual licence. You know the number before you talk to anyone — $2,199 for Small, $3,799 for Company, $6,499 for Enterprise — you install it on your own Windows servers, and it runs indefinitely. One year of upgrades and support is bundled; after that, staying current means renewing maintenance, which the "one-time" framing quietly omits.

Deskpro's answer is Deskpro Private, quoted case by case with no published price. If data sovereignty is why you're evaluating Deskpro, nothing on the cloud pricing page tells you what you'll pay, and that conversation should happen early rather than after you've fallen for the product.

That difference in transparency shapes how long procurement takes and how much room there is to negotiate.

## The cloud economics are not close

Jitbit prices flat within an agent band. Twenty agents on a mainstream per-seat competitor at $50 is $1,000 a month; Jitbit's top published cloud tier is $249. Deskpro at twenty agents on Professional is $1,180.

Deskpro's structure has a specific hazard: the seat minimums are the pricing model. Six agents on Team pay $234. That same team upgrading to Professional for AI and multi-brand support pays for ten seats — $590 a month, with four seats empty. The per-agent rate went up 51%; the bill went up 152%. Teams of six to nine sit in a genuine dead zone above Team's comfort and below Professional's minimum.

Jitbit has its own boundary problem, milder but real. Adding one agent at the wrong moment moves you from $69 to $129 in the cloud, or from a $2,199 licence to a $3,799 one. Know where your bands fall before you hire.

## What the money buys on each side

Deskpro consolidates email, live chat, phone, Facebook, SMS and more into one queue, with routing rules, triggers, macros, escalations and SLA enforcement all configurable without code, 1,000+ integrations, and customizable roles. Deskpro AI and the AI chatbot are included from Professional rather than metered — genuinely unusual, and it materially improves the case for the $59 tier. Lite Agents are the underrated part: 50 on Professional, 1,000 on Enterprise, letting engineers and product managers view and collaborate on tickets without full seats.

Jitbit is deliberately conventional. Email-to-ticket conversion, IMAP, Exchange, SMTP and Office 365 support, automation rules on if-this-then-that logic, a knowledge base, asset tracking, and Jira and GitHub integrations that developer teams appreciate. HIPAA compliance with a BAA is available on Enterprise.

What Jitbit lacks is decisive for some buyers: no native live chat or in-product messaging, and no AI deflection layer at all. If your business case is "the AI resolves 40% of tickets," Jitbit isn't competing in that category and the price comparison is meaningless. If your priority is a dependable system of record with nothing metered and no credits to track, that absence is the point.

## Costing self-hosting honestly

Jitbit's licence looks like it pays back in about thirty months — $3,799 against $129 a month is 29 months on the raw numbers. It doesn't stay that clean. Add maintenance renewal for years two through five, Windows Server and SQL Server licensing if you don't already own it, hosting, TLS certificates, patching cadence on a web-facing app handling customer data, and backup with restore testing. Realistic break-even lands closer to three or four years.

Still a clear win for a stable IT department running the same system for a decade; not a win for a company that will replace its help desk twice in that period.

## Who should pick what

- **Regulated organizations already running Windows infrastructure with a sysadmin** → Jitbit self-hosted. The published licence price and full data control are hard to beat.
- **Support teams handling phone, chat, SMS and social alongside email** → Deskpro. Jitbit simply doesn't do those channels.
- **Fifteen to fifty agents with a wide pool of internal collaborators** → Deskpro Professional. Fifty Lite Agents changes the comparison against per-seat competitors entirely.
- **Larger email-only teams tired of per-seat billing** → Jitbit cloud. $249 a month at the top band is exceptional value if the feature gap doesn't bite.
- **Teams of six to nine agents** → buy Deskpro Team and stay there, or look at Jitbit. Early upgrading into Professional's ten-seat minimum is one of the more expensive mistakes available here.
- **Anyone needing HIPAA-compliant hosting under 25 agents** → Deskpro Enterprise costs $2,475 a month regardless; check whether Jitbit Enterprise's BAA covers your requirement first.

## Bottom line

Jitbit is the disciplined choice when your ticket flow is email, your horizon is long, and you'd rather own the infrastructure than rent the software. Its price stability in a category that has moved to metered AI is a real feature, not a limitation, for the right buyer. Deskpro is the choice when support genuinely arrives through five channels and workflow complexity needs modeling — but price the seat minimums, not the per-agent rate, and open the Deskpro Private conversation before you get attached.

Both vendors adjust bands and minimums. Confirm current numbers at jitbit.com and deskpro.com, and ask Jitbit specifically what year-two maintenance renewal costs before treating the licence as a one-time expense.
