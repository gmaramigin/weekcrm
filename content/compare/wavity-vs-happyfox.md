## TL;DR

- Wavity publishes everything: $25 Professional, $40 Premium, $55 Enterprise per agent annually, five-agent minimum at every tier.
- HappyFox publishes nothing. Basic caps at 5 agents, higher plans go unlimited, annual saves 20%, and there is a second flat-fee model priced against a yearly ticket allowance.
- Wavity's differentiator is the no-code Application Builder and ITSM at mid-market rates. HappyFox's is maturity, dual customer-support and IT-desk use, and unusually strong G2 standing against Zendesk.
- Both charge extra for AI, and neither publishes what it costs.

## Transparency is the first real difference

Wavity's rate card is the kind you can budget against before anyone calls you: three tiers, published annual and monthly rates, a stated five-agent floor.

HappyFox has moved the other way. Its pricing pages describe the mechanism carefully — per agent, monthly or annual, 20% off for annual, Basic capped at five agents, unlimited above — then route you to a demo form. Third-party roundups publish figures, but they contradict each other by wide margins, so anchoring on them is guesswork dressed as research.

That asymmetry decides who each product wins. A five-person IT team comparing four tools on a spreadsheet can price Wavity and cannot price HappyFox, so HappyFox loses that shortlist by default. A mid-market buyer with a procurement function was going to have a sales conversation anyway.

## Wavity's tier trap

Professional at $25 includes core ticketing, basic routing, incident management, asset management, a mobile app, and analytics. It does not include SLA management, the knowledge base, or the customer portal — all three start at Premium, $40.

Those omissions are load-bearing. SLA tracking is often the reason a desk bought software rather than kept using a shared inbox, and knowledge base plus portal are the deflection layer that reduces ticket volume instead of just recording it. Buying without them means paying per agent for a system that does nothing to lower how many agents you need.

Add the five-agent minimum and the realistic floor is five seats at Premium: $2,400 a year. For a three-person internal IT team, the effective rate on Professional is $42 per real agent, not $25.

## HappyFox's two pricing models

HappyFox's more interesting structural feature is a genuine alternative to per-agent billing: a flat annual fee sized against a yearly ticket allowance, no per-seat charge. The vendor itself suggests this becomes the better deal somewhere above 20 to 25 agents.

The models reward opposite shapes. A 30-person team handling complex, low-volume enterprise tickets is punished by per-agent pricing and should push for the flat plan. A six-person team drowning in consumer volume is the reverse. Work out your tickets-per-agent-per-month before the call and you will know which to ask for rather than being steered. HappyFox also removes the per-seat penalty on live chat plans, which is unusual at its price point and matters for teams staffing chat with a large rotating pool.

## ITSM depth versus support-desk breadth

Wavity's Enterprise tier at $55 is where its ambition shows: change management, problem management, CMDB, service catalog, live chat, advanced analytics, workflow designer, and Teams/Slack integration. Those are ITIL disciplines, not conveniences, and $55 per agent is aggressive against ServiceNow or Ivanti for the same coverage. Layered on top is the no-code Application Builder for custom apps, forms, and workflows, plus wBots for robotic process automation. For a team with unusual internal processes and no developers to spare, that builder is the reason to shortlist Wavity at all.

HappyFox runs external customer support and an internal IT service desk from one product, across education, ecommerce, healthcare, telecom, and government. Its automation covers routing, escalations, SLA enforcement, and scheduled tasks, and its G2 standing sits consistently ahead of Zendesk and Salesforce Service Cloud on ease of use and support quality. It is the safer, more proven choice; Wavity is the less well-known one, with fewer community resources and a smaller integration catalog.

## The AI line item nobody prices

Both vendors put AI outside the base licence, and neither publishes what it costs. Wavity's Agentic AI suite — chatbots, automated resolution, sentiment analysis, AI knowledge base — is quoted by sales. HappyFox splits AI across separate SKUs entirely: Autopilot, Assist AI, and Chatbot are distinct products, not features of the help desk licence.

That creates the same evaluation error at both. The demo shows the assembled platform; the quote covers one piece of it. HappyFox goes further still, selling Help Desk, Service Desk, AI Contact Center, CRM, and Workflows separately — so ask explicitly which SKUs are in scope before comparing a quote to a competitor's all-in tier. Wavity's narrower caution: its CRM module is basic next to a dedicated sales CRM, and advanced workflow automation modules are unpriced too.

## Who should pick what

- **Small IT teams (5–15 agents) that need a price today** → Wavity Premium at $40, the only one you can budget without a call.
- **Mid-market desks above 25 agents with low ticket volume per agent** → HappyFox, and push for the flat-fee unlimited-agent plan.
- **Teams needing formal ITIL change, problem, and CMDB on a mid-market budget** → Wavity Enterprise at $55.
- **Organizations running customer support and internal IT from one tool** → HappyFox, built for that dual case.
- **Teams with unusual internal processes and no developers** → Wavity, for the no-code Application Builder.
- **Anyone frustrated by Zendesk's pricing or complexity** → HappyFox, the more established alternative and better rated on ease of use.
- **Desks under five agents** → neither. Wavity's minimum inflates your effective rate by 70% or more, and HappyFox Basic caps at five so the sixth hire reprices the whole team.

## Bottom line

Wavity is the better-priced product for small and mid-sized IT teams that want ITSM capability without ITSM procurement — provided you budget Premium rather than the advertised $25 and account for the five-agent floor. Annual billing saves 22–25% here, above the category norm, so take the term if headcount is stable. HappyFox is the more proven platform and the more flexible commercial partner, especially for large teams whose ticket volume is modest relative to headcount, but you cannot evaluate it from a webpage and its AI capabilities are separate purchases. Decide first whether you can tolerate a sales-only process; that constraint eliminates one of these two for most small teams before any feature comparison starts.
