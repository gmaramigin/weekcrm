## TL;DR

- Odoo Helpdesk is free forever for unlimited users — as long as it is the only Odoo app you touch. Activate a second app and the whole company moves to $7.25/user/month.
- Zoho Desk is free for three agents, then $7 Express, $14 Standard, $23 Professional, $40 Enterprise, per agent, annual.
- Odoo's advantage is ERP context: tickets sit in the same database as invoices, inventory, and sales orders. Zoho's advantage is being an actual support product, with depth Odoo's module does not attempt.
- Odoo licensing is cheap and Odoo implementations are not. Budget the partner engagement separately or the comparison is meaningless.

## Two free tiers built on opposite logic

Odoo's One App Free plan is the most generous offer in this category and it is not a trial. Full Helpdesk, unlimited users, hosted and maintained by Odoo, at no cost, permanently. No agent cap, no feature stripping, no clock. For a company that wants ticketing and genuinely nothing else, nothing in the dedicated help desk market competes with zero.

Zoho's free plan is narrower but shaped differently: three agents forever, with email ticketing, a knowledge base, a customer-facing help center, mobile apps, and basic reporting. Fewer people, but you are not forbidden from using Zoho CRM or Zoho Books alongside it.

That distinction is the whole comparison in miniature. Odoo restricts *breadth*; Zoho restricts *headcount*. Which limit you hit first depends on whether your support team is large or your software footprint is.

## Pricing

Odoo prices per user, not per agent, once you cross from one app to two. Standard is $7.25 per user per month annually ($8.95 monthly) and includes every app Odoo makes — CRM, Sales, Inventory, Accounting, Projects, and hundreds more. Custom is $10.90 annually ($13.60 monthly) and unlocks Odoo Studio, external API access, multi-company support, and hosting on Odoo.sh or on-premise.

Zoho Desk prices per support agent: $7 Express, $14 Standard, $23 Professional, $40 Enterprise, all annual. Monthly billing runs roughly a third higher. Live chat sits at Enterprise, which is the sharpest step in the lineup.

Now the part that changes the math. A 60-person company with 8 support agents pays Zoho for 8 seats — $1,344 a year on Standard. The same company on Odoo, once it activates CRM alongside Helpdesk, pays for all 60 users: $5,220 a year. But that $5,220 buys the CRM, the accounting, the inventory, and everything else, replacing several other subscriptions.

Compare like for like and Odoo is cheap. Compare Odoo's suite bill against Zoho Desk's help desk bill and Odoo looks expensive. Both readings are correct; you have to decide which purchase you are actually making.

## The context each one gives an agent

This is where the products genuinely differ rather than just pricing differently.

Odoo Helpdesk lives inside an ERP. A ticket links natively to the customer's sales order, the invoice they are disputing, the inventory item they are waiting on, and the project task your team opened about it — one database, no connectors. For a business that ships physical goods or bills complex invoices, that context is the reason to buy: agents stop asking other departments questions and start reading the answer.

Zoho Desk gives a different kind of context — two-way sync with Zoho CRM, so agents see sales history and account status alongside each ticket. Excellent where "context" means the relationship rather than the order. It also covers channels Odoo's module does not seriously attempt: social, community forums, telephony at Professional, live chat at Enterprise.

If your support questions are "where is my shipment" and "why does this invoice say that," Odoo wins. If they are "how do I do this" and "can you escalate," Zoho does.

## Customization, hosting, and the bill nobody plans for

Odoo's flexibility is real but it is gated. Odoo Studio, the tool for modifying models and workflows without a developer, requires the Custom tier — a 50% per-user uplift across every employee. External API access sits there too, so any programmatic integration with a non-Odoo system forces the same jump. And Standard is Odoo Online only, meaning a preference for on-premise hosting alone lands you at $10.90. Teams that buy Standard expecting to run stock configuration usually discover, a quarter in, that they need one thing changed.

Then implementation. Success Packs and certified partner engagements are quoted separately at every Odoo tier and routinely exceed the first year of licensing for anything beyond a single-app setup. The subscription is the smallest line in a serious Odoo budget.

Zoho Desk's configuration surface is dense but self-serve. Small teams sometimes find it heavier than the problem warrants, and buyer reviews repeatedly flag renewal increases after promotional pricing lapses — get the year-two rate in writing. But there is no implied partner engagement, and nobody needs a consultant to launch it.

## Who should pick what

- **Standalone ticketing with a $0 budget** → Odoo Helpdesk on One App Free. Unlimited users, hosted, permanent. Nothing else is close.
- **Businesses already running or planning Odoo ERP** → Odoo Helpdesk, clearly. The native links to orders, stock, and invoices are the entire value.
- **Companies on Zoho CRM or Zoho One** → Zoho Desk, same logic in reverse.
- **Support teams needing multi-channel depth — social, telephony, chat** → Zoho Desk, and price Enterprise honestly if chat matters.
- **Small support team inside a large company** → Zoho Desk. Odoo bills every employee once you pass one app; Zoho never bills anyone who is not an agent.
- **Anyone comparing on total cost of ownership** → get an Odoo partner quote before deciding. The $7.25 figure is real and it is not the project cost.

## Bottom line

These are both suite products where the help desk is bait for the ecosystem, and both are honest about it. Odoo Helpdesk is the stronger choice for operations-heavy businesses whose support questions are really supply chain and billing questions, and its free tier is a legitimate reason to start there even if you never go further. Zoho Desk is the better *support* product — more channels, more automation aimed at support work specifically, and a per-agent meter that does not punish you for the size of the rest of the company. Pick Odoo if you are buying an ERP. Pick Zoho Desk if you are buying a help desk.
