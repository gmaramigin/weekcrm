## TL;DR

- The two products are mirror images. LiveHelpNow starts as a chat tool ($39 Essential) and charges $59 Professional to add ticketing. Zoho Desk starts as a ticketing tool ($14 Standard) and charges $40 Enterprise to add live chat.
- Zoho Desk publishes per-agent rates you can multiply by headcount. LiveHelpNow publishes "from" rates that scale with agents and usage, so you cannot price it without a sales call.
- LiveHelpNow's AI is a separate, expensive rate card — $99 to $799 a month on top of the base plan. Zoho folds its AI into Enterprise.
- Zoho Desk carries the higher rating here (4.2 vs 4.0), and a permanent free tier for three agents that has no equivalent on the LiveHelpNow side.

## Which channel is your center of gravity?

Everything downstream follows from this. If most of your volume arrives as live chat on a website or storefront, LiveHelpNow's packaging works with you: chat, knowledge base, support portal, surveys, canned responses, and secure forms all sit in the $39 entry tier. Zoho Desk would make you climb to $40 per agent for that same capability, since live chat is an Enterprise feature — the single largest jump in its lineup, from $23 to $40.

If most of your volume arrives as email, the arithmetic flips hard. Zoho Desk's Standard tier at $14 per agent gives you multi-channel ticketing, social and community channels, SLAs, and dashboards. LiveHelpNow has no ticketing at all below Professional, so the realistic starting price for an email-handling desk there is $59.

Very few teams are purely one or the other, which is why most buyers end up on LiveHelpNow Professional or Zoho Desk Standard/Professional rather than either entry plan.

## Pricing

Zoho Desk, annual billing: Free for three agents, Express $7/agent, Standard $14, Professional $23, Enterprise $40. Monthly billing runs materially higher — independent trackers put Standard nearer $20 and Enterprise nearer $50.

LiveHelpNow: Essential from $39/month, Professional from $59, Premium from $79 — none of them per agent, all of them prefixed "from." That prefix does real work. A five-person desk and a fifteen-person desk both quote "from $59" and both get invoiced differently, so the rate card is a floor rather than a forecast.

Then the AI layer, which is where LiveHelpNow's bill genuinely diverges. AI Agent Assist is free but internal-only, helping agents draft rather than talking to customers. Knowledge AI starts at $99/month, Conversational AI at $499, Conversational AI Plus at $799. Conversational AI costs roughly eight times the Professional plan it attaches to, and it meters by conversation volume — so a busy month, an outage, or a campaign drives the bill up for reasons your headcount planning never sees.

Zoho's AI sits inside Enterprise pricing instead. Less granular, but predictable.

## Ecosystem versus independence

Zoho Desk's strongest argument has never been the help desk in isolation — it is the two-way sync with Zoho CRM, where support agents and sales reps share one customer record without middleware. If your company already runs Zoho CRM or Zoho One, that integration is most of the reason to buy, and the $14 Standard tier becomes hard to argue against on value.

Invert that and the argument weakens noticeably. A team whose CRM lives elsewhere and who needs live chat as a primary channel is paying $40 per agent for Enterprise while collecting none of the ecosystem benefit. In that specific scenario, LiveHelpNow deserves a real look, as do Freshdesk and Help Scout.

LiveHelpNow has no comparable ecosystem play. What it offers instead is multi-brand support from a single account — running support for several properties or brands without separate subscriptions. Zoho Desk gates multi-brand help centers behind Enterprise.

## Transparency and evaluation friction

You can build a Zoho Desk budget in a spreadsheet in five minutes: agents times tier, annual or monthly, done. Twenty agents on Standard is $3,360 a year and nothing about that number is surprising. LiveHelpNow cannot be evaluated that way — every figure is a starting point, so any comparison against per-agent competitors requires a written quote for your actual agent count first. That is a procurement cost, not just an inconvenience.

One more wrinkle on the LiveHelpNow side: removing its branding from the chat widget requires the $79 Premium plan. Consumer brands and agencies presenting to clients often find that single item decides the tier regardless of features.

## Who should pick what

- **Chat-first ecommerce or consumer sites** → LiveHelpNow Professional. Chat depth at the bottom of the stack, ticketing included, no $40/agent toll for real-time conversation.
- **Anyone running Zoho CRM or Zoho One** → Zoho Desk, without much debate. The shared record is the product.
- **Small teams testing the water** → Zoho Desk Free. Three agents, permanently, with real email ticketing and a help center. LiveHelpNow has no equivalent.
- **Multi-brand support operations on a budget** → LiveHelpNow, since Zoho puts multi-brand help centers on Enterprise.
- **Teams needing conversational AI deflection at modest volume** → neither at first. LiveHelpNow's $499 entry only pencils out above roughly a thousand conversations a month; start with Knowledge AI at $99 or Zoho's Enterprise AI.
- **Buyers who must compare vendors on published numbers** → Zoho Desk, by process of elimination.

## Bottom line

Zoho Desk is the safer default: cheaper at every equivalent tier, transparently priced, better rated, and free to start. Its weak spot is live chat, which costs $40 per agent and reframes the whole comparison for chat-heavy teams. LiveHelpNow is the specialist answer for those teams — strong chat, multi-brand handling, ticketing from $59 — but you will need a quote before you can compare it to anything, and its AI is priced as a separate product rather than a feature. Decide your dominant channel first, then price only the tier that actually contains it.
