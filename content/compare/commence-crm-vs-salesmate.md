## TL;DR

- **Commence CRM** ladders $12 (Essentials), $19 (Sales), $32 (Enterprise) per user annually, then charges $12, $12, and $10 per user for Marketing, Customer Service, and Project Management modules.
- **Salesmate** ladders $23 (Basic), $39 (Pro), $63 (Business) per user monthly, 20% off annually, with 5K–15K automation credits per user per month baked into each tier.
- Salesmate rates 4.3 here, Commence 3.8. The gap shows up in interface, integrations, and compliance posture rather than raw feature count.
- Commence is cheaper at almost every configuration. Whether that matters depends on whether your team dials, texts, and runs sequences.

## Both sell consolidation, from different decades

Commence Corporation's product is a broad SMB system: accounts, contacts, segmentation, opportunity pipelines, forecasting, territory and quota management, quoting and pricebook, ticketing, and multi-project management with Gantt charts and time tracking. It targets manufacturers, distributors, and professional services firms tracking both a deal and the delivery work that follows. Nobody claims it is pretty — the interface feels dated and the integration catalog is narrow.

Salesmate is the newer approach to the same problem. Contacts, deals, marketing, help desk, and communications share one data layer, with 700-plus integrations, SOC 2, GDPR, HIPAA, and ISO 27001 certifications, and AI co-pilots that transcribe calls and summarize conversations. Its top verticals — real estate, insurance, retail, manufacturing, SaaS — are teams where phone and text are part of the daily motion.

That last point is the cleanest separator. Salesmate is built for teams that talk to people; Commence is built for teams that track them.

## Both headline rates undersell the real number

Commence's $12 tier has no opportunity tracking. No pipeline, no deal stages, no forecasting. It is a contact and activity manager, and the first tier a salesperson would recognize is $19. Add Marketing and you are at $31 per seat; a fully configured Enterprise seat carrying all three modules lands at $66. Crucially, modules bill across every seat on the account, not just the people who use them, so support ticketing for two staff in a ten-person firm still costs $120 a month. There is a three-user minimum and no advertised monthly billing.

Salesmate's version of the same problem is credits and telephony. Every tier meters workflow executions, sequence steps, and AI actions against a monthly allowance — 5,000 per user on Basic, 10,000 on Pro, 15,000 on Business — and heavy sequence users buy top-up packs. Basic has no sequences at all, so outbound teams start at Pro. The built-in phone system is a marketed feature but the minutes and numbers are billed separately, and the power dialer with voicemail drop sits only on Business at $63. Enterprise onboarding starts at $1,999.

Priced honestly, a ten-person outbound team runs about $312 a month on annual Pro before telephony, and around $504 on Business with the dialer. The comparable Commence configuration — ten seats on Sales plus Marketing — is $310, with no dialer available at any price.

## Where Salesmate justifies the premium

Native calling and SMS inside the record, with AI transcription and summaries attached, changes what a rep's day looks like. No separate dialer, no copy-pasted call notes, no gap between the conversation and the CRM. Commence has nothing equivalent, and stitching a phone system to it means leaning on an integration ecosystem the vendor itself is not strong in.

The compliance certifications matter more than they sound. HIPAA and ISO 27001 open doors in healthcare, insurance, and regulated finance that a smaller vendor without them cannot walk through, regardless of features.

Salesmate also has no permanent free tier, which is worth knowing going in — evaluation is a time-limited trial, so plan it around a real pipeline rather than a sandbox.

## Where Commence holds up

Quoting and pricebook at $32 is a real feature that plenty of mid-market CRMs charge more for. The Project Management module at $10 is the best-value item on either vendor's list, because the alternative is a second subscription and a synchronization headache. And for a firm that genuinely wants ticketing, projects, and CRM from one supplier at a small budget, $19 to $32 per seat undercuts almost everything with comparable configurability.

The caveat is that Commence rewards patience. Configuring it takes work, the interface will not delight anyone, and if your evaluation criteria include "will reps actually open this," that deserves weight.

## Who should pick what

- **Outbound teams doing volume calling and texting** → Salesmate Business, budgeting telephony on top.
- **Regulated industries needing HIPAA or ISO 27001** → Salesmate; Commence does not compete here.
- **Distributors and manufacturers needing quotes, pricebooks, and territories** → Commence Enterprise.
- **Firms that bill project work alongside sales** → Commence, for the Gantt and time-tracking module at $10.
- **Small teams wanting sequences without a big spend** → Salesmate Pro at roughly $31 annually beats assembling it on Commence.
- **Companies where only a few staff need a given function** → Commence penalizes you for this; price Salesmate instead.

## Bottom line

Salesmate is the better-built product and the one most sales teams will prefer using, with communications and AI that Commence simply does not offer. Commence wins on cost and on unusual breadth — projects, quoting, ticketing — for organizations that value coverage over experience and have the patience to configure it. Model the real configuration, not the entry tier, on both sides: Commence's modules and Salesmate's credits are where quotes drift away from the pricing page.
