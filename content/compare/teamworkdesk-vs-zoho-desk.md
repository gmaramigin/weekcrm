## TL;DR

- Teamwork Desk exists to turn a client email into tracked project work. Starter runs $9.50/agent/month annually but caps hard at 10 agents.
- Zoho Desk is a broader help desk — social, community, telephony, multi-department routing — free for three agents, $14 on Standard, $40 on Enterprise.
- Neither one wins on a spec sheet. Teamwork Desk wins when your support tickets turn into billable delivery work. Zoho Desk wins when support is its own function with its own channels.

## Two different definitions of a ticket

Teamwork Desk treats a ticket as the front end of project work. An account manager gets a client email, it lands in the desk, and one click makes it a task on the project with time tracking and budget attached. That handoff is the entire product thesis, and everything else in Teamwork Desk — the client portals, the per-client support tiers, the triggers — feeds it.

Zoho Desk treats a ticket as a support conversation that needs to be resolved, measured, and ideally deflected before it arrives. It unifies email, chat, phone, social, and web forms into one queue, layers CRM context onto each record, and adds generative AI for sentiment, summarization, and reply drafts. Nothing in it assumes the resolution becomes chargeable work downstream.

That difference is not cosmetic. An agency that resolves half its tickets by doing three hours of billable development is badly served by a desk that closes the ticket and forgets. A SaaS support team fielding 3,000 emails a month is badly served by a desk built around project handoff.

## Pricing

Teamwork Desk publishes $9.50 (Starter), $17 (Pro), and $33 (Premium) per agent per month on annual terms, with a custom Enterprise tier for HIPAA verification. Annual saves 29%, which is above the usual 20% norm. The trap is structural: Starter allows a maximum of 10 agents. Agent eleven forces the whole desk to Pro, taking a team from $95/month to $187 — roughly double the bill for one hire.

Zoho Desk is free forever for three agents, then $7 (Express), $14 (Standard), $23 (Professional), and $40 (Enterprise) per agent annually. No agent caps in the middle of the ladder. Its equivalent trap is channel placement: live chat is an Enterprise feature at $40, and light agent licenses only come free at Enterprise, listed at 50.

Compare like for like and the picture shifts by requirement. If you want live chat, Teamwork Desk asks $17 and Zoho Desk asks $40 — Teamwork is dramatically cheaper. If you want SLAs and real reporting, Teamwork asks $33 while Zoho includes both at $14 — Zoho is dramatically cheaper. There is no consistent winner, only a per-feature one.

## Channel coverage and self-service

Zoho Desk has the wider channel mix by a distance: social, community, telephony at Professional, and web forms alongside email and chat. Teamwork Desk covers email, live chat from Pro, and a customer portal. If your clients reach you through social or a public community forum, Teamwork Desk has no lane for that.

Self-service is closer. Teamwork Desk includes a free help docs site at Starter, and Zoho puts a knowledge base and help center on its free plan, so both entry tiers start deflecting immediately. Zoho pulls ahead at Enterprise with multi-brand help centers.

## Ecosystem gravity

Both products are worth much less outside their parent suites, and buyers underprice this.

Teamwork Desk without Teamwork.com projects is a mid-sized help desk with a narrow integration catalog competing against Freshdesk and Help Scout on ordinary merit. It holds up at Pro, but the differentiation evaporates. Teamwork's own materials lean on the project tie because that is the argument.

Zoho Desk without Zoho CRM loses its two-way sync, which is the reason its $14 Standard tier reads as such good value. You are still buying a competent multi-channel desk, but you are paying for an integration advantage you never collect, and the comparison against Freshdesk gets uncomfortably close.

## Where each one gets awkward

Teamwork Desk's ladder puts service commitments expensively out of reach. SLAs, satisfaction ratings, custom fields, business hours, and full reporting all sit at Premium — $33 per agent, 3.5x the headline. Any agency that has promised a client a response time is looking at the top published tier. HIPAA verification is Enterprise-only.

Zoho Desk's awkwardness is interface density and renewal behavior. Small teams sometimes find the configuration surface heavier than their problem warrants, and buyer reviews flag renewal increases after promotional pricing lapses often enough that getting the year-two rate in writing is basic hygiene.

## Who should pick what

- **Agencies and services firms already on Teamwork.com** → Teamwork Desk, no contest. Ticket-to-task continuity is worth more than any feature Zoho lists.
- **Support teams under three agents with no budget** → Zoho Desk Free. It is a working desk, not a trial.
- **Teams that owe customers documented SLAs on a tight budget** → Zoho Desk Standard at $14 includes what Teamwork charges $33 for.
- **Small teams that need live chat cheaply** → Teamwork Desk Pro at $17 beats Zoho's Enterprise-gated chat at $40.
- **Anyone running Zoho CRM** → Zoho Desk, for the shared customer record alone.
- **Teams supporting multiple products or departments** → Zoho Desk Professional; Teamwork Desk has no equivalent routing model.

## Bottom line

Model your own tier before you compare headlines. Teamwork Desk's $9.50 is real but only for ten agents doing email; the price you will actually pay depends on whether you need chat ($17) or SLAs ($33). Zoho Desk's $14 is real and generous, but live chat pushes you to $40, which changes the math entirely. The clean version of this decision: if your support work becomes project work, buy Teamwork Desk and accept the narrower channel set. If support is a standalone function — especially one sitting next to Zoho CRM — buy Zoho Desk and take Standard until multi-department or chat forces your hand.
