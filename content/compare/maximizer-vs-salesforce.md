## TL;DR

- **Maximizer** publishes three tiers — $65 Core, $79 mid, $89 "+" — across two parallel editions, Sales and Financial Services. The edition matters more than the tier, and you cannot swap lines later without a migration.
- **Salesforce Sales Cloud** lists $25 Starter, $100 Pro, $175 Enterprise, $350 Unlimited, $550 Agentforce 1. Enterprise is where serious deployments land, and a 25-rep year one realistically clears $200,000 once implementation and an admin are counted.
- Maximizer still sells on-premise. In 2026 that is close to unique in this category, and for some regulated buyers it ends the evaluation immediately.

## Two different kinds of purchase

Salesforce is not sold as finished software. It ships as a platform — custom objects, flows, validation rules, Apex — and somebody has to model your business inside it before a rep logs in. That is why the pricing page understates the cost so badly: partner implementation commonly runs one to three times annual license value, and the administrator the platform requires is a permanent salary line no tier includes.

Maximizer is sold as a configured product. Its Financial Services edition arrives already shaped for wealth advisors, insurance agents, and credit unions — investment and insurance data access, household and family connection mapping, compliance-oriented workflows. You are buying somebody else's opinion about how an advisory practice runs, which is either exactly what you want or exactly what you resent.

## Pricing shapes, compared honestly

Maximizer's ladder spans just 37% from bottom to top. That flatness is a real virtue: choosing the "+" tier from day one is a modest premium rather than a budget fight, and most firms should just do that. The tier trap is Core — at $65 you get contacts, tasks, reminders, interaction history, mobile, and Outlook sync, with no pipeline visualization, no dashboards, and no real-time reporting. Buyers who want to see their pipeline are $79 customers.

Salesforce's ladder spans 22x, and each rung means something different. Starter at $25 is price-competitive but deliberately narrowed — you get the brand without the customization depth that is the reason to choose Salesforce in the first place. Pro at $100 rarely justifies quadrupling spend for a small team. Enterprise at $175 is the tier that makes the platform a platform: sandboxes, full API, unrestricted automation. Everything above that is a support-and-AI decision.

Worth noting for international buyers: Maximizer's regional prices are not straight conversions. The "+" tier is $89 USD but CAD $125 and AUD $129, a premium worth raising in negotiation. Salesforce discounts are negotiable too, and the more valuable win is capping the annual uplift clause rather than shaving points off the headline.

## Where regulated and relationship-led work lands

If your CRM is a book-of-business asset — households, policies, portfolios, decades of interaction history — Maximizer's Financial Services line is built for that shape and Salesforce is not, at least not without Financial Services Cloud and a configuration project on top. Maximizer's family connection visualization has no direct equivalent in stock Sales Cloud.

The deployment question cuts the same way. Maximizer quotes a self-hosted license for organizations facing data residency rules or regulators who will not accept multi-tenant cloud. Salesforce has no equivalent answer. Be clear-eyed about what self-hosting costs beyond the license — infrastructure, backups, patching, upgrades, and the staff time to run them — but when compliance rules out cloud, economics stop being the deciding argument.

## Complexity, ecosystem, and the talent question

Salesforce wins decisively on breadth. AppExchange carries thousands of prebuilt integrations, Einstein and Agentforce are mature, and the admin and consultant talent pool means hiring is never the bottleneck. If your business has multiple sales motions, territory and commission modeling, or a roadmap you cannot yet describe, that headroom is worth paying for.

It is also the cost. AppExchange packages for e-signature, dialing, CPQ, or enrichment are separately licensed and routinely add $30 to $80 per seat. AI runs on metered Flex Credits at $500 per 100,000, unrelated to your seat count. Maximizer has none of that surface area — the repo notes its integration ecosystem and interface both feel dated next to modern tools, and advanced visualization and expanded storage are extra purchases.

## Contract terms nobody reads until renewal

Salesforce bills monthly only on Starter. Pro, Enterprise, Unlimited, and Agentforce are annual commitments on contracted seats — hire twelve reps, lose four in month five, and you pay for twelve until renewal. Multi-year deals commonly embed automatic escalators on top of list increases.

Maximizer publishes per-user monthly rates across five currencies with an Enterprise tier quoted for custom development, data lake integration, and an SLA. It is a simpler commercial relationship, and for a fifteen-person advisory firm that simplicity has value in itself.

## Who should pick what

- **Wealth advisors, insurance agencies, credit unions** → Maximizer Financial Services, and pick the line before the tier.
- **Any organization that cannot use multi-tenant cloud** → Maximizer, for the on-premise quote.
- **Sales orgs past roughly 100 reps with real process complexity** → Salesforce Enterprise, budgeted at 4x the license line for year one.
- **Teams that want pipeline dashboards on a small budget** → neither; Maximizer's mid tier at $79 and Salesforce Pro at $100 are both expensive for that job.
- **Companies with no admin capacity and no appetite for a 4-to-12-week rollout** → Maximizer, or a lighter SMB tool entirely.

## Bottom line

Comparing these on per-seat price produces a wrong answer. Maximizer at $89 all-in is a predictable number; Salesforce at $175 is the opening line of a four-part budget. Choose Maximizer when your requirements are relationship depth, regulatory posture, or deployment control, and you want the software to arrive already shaped. Choose Salesforce when process complexity and ecosystem headroom genuinely justify employing someone to run it — and if you cannot name that person, you are not ready to buy it.
