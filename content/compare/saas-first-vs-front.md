## TL;DR

- **SaaS First** is a single $9/member/month plan containing the inbox, AI chatbot, CRM, boards, analytics and marketing campaigns. Each seat brings 100 AI credits; extra conversations cost $0.09.
- **Front** starts at $25/seat/month but that tier allows one channel type and ten seats. Real omnichannel begins at Professional, $65/seat/month, and the AI products are priced separately on top.
- Rated 3.9 and 4.2 respectively in our directory, and the ratings understate how differently they behave. One is a consolidation play for lean teams; the other is coordination software for operations that span departments.

## The pricing shapes have nothing in common

Comparing seat rates here is close to meaningless, because the two vendors meter different things.

SaaS First charges $9 per member, one plan, no feature tiering, and grants 100 AI credits per seat — one credit covering an entire chatbot conversation inside a 24-hour window. The included credits are described as worth exactly $9, which tells you where the vendor thinks the value sits. Model this product on conversation volume: 5,000 chatbot conversations a month on a five-person team is roughly $450, of which only $45 is seats.

Front charges per seat and gates channel types. Starter's $25 covers email only and caps at ten people. Adding SMS or WhatsApp moves you to Professional at $65 — a 2.6x jump per seat that is not an upgrade fee but a different plan. Professional itself caps at fifty seats. Enterprise lists at $105. Front's AI is unbundled: Copilot $20/seat, Smart QA $20/seat, Smart CSAT $10/seat, Autopilot metered from $0.05 per conversation. Buy two or more of those on Professional and you cross Enterprise's list price, where they ship included.

Annual billing takes 24% off Front, one of the steeper discounts in the category. SaaS First has a 14-day trial and no negotiating surface at all — a single published plan means no volume rate to ask for.

## Who the AI is actually for

Both products lead with AI, and they mean different things by it.

SaaS First's Milly is a customer-facing chatbot: multilingual, always on, meant to answer before a human sees the thread. It is included in the subscription and metered by conversation, which suits a company whose main support burden is repetitive website chat.

Front's AI splits between deflection (Autopilot, claimed to handle up to 70% of inbound on qualified plans) and agent assistance (Copilot drafting, Smart QA scoring conversations, Smart CSAT measuring outcomes). The QA and CSAT tooling in particular is aimed at support managers running a team large enough to need sampling and quality scores — a problem a five-person startup does not have.

If nobody at your company is auditing conversation quality, half of Front's AI catalogue is spend you will not use.

## Coordination versus consolidation

This is the real dividing line. Front exists because in logistics, freight, financial services and wholesale, one customer request touches sales, fulfillment and support before it closes, and email chains lose the thread. Front keeps the conversation intact across those handoffs, with routing, SLAs and 160+ integrations into CRMs and ERPs. Around 9,000 companies use it, mostly mid-market and up.

SaaS First exists because a small SaaS company otherwise pays for a chat tool, a help desk, a light CRM and a marketing emailer separately. Bundling those at $9 removes four invoices. The tradeoff is stated plainly in its own listing: each module is deliberately shallow next to a dedicated tool, the integration ecosystem is small, and brand recognition is limited.

Neither weakness matters to the other's buyer. A twelve-person SaaS team does not need cross-department routing. A freight brokerage does not want its CRM to be a side module.

## Practical limits to check before you commit

- Front's seat caps can force two plan changes as you grow from eight agents to sixty, each with a price step you did not choose. Ask about them early.
- Front has no cheap read-only seat, so stakeholders who glance at a thread weekly cost the same as your busiest agent.
- SaaS First ties AI allowance to headcount while consumption is driven by traffic. A two-person team with a busy site gets 200 included conversations and may need thousands.
- Confirm whether SaaS First credits roll over month to month. If they reset, quiet months subsidize nothing.

## Who should pick what

- **Early-stage SaaS wanting one bill instead of four** → SaaS First. At $9 a seat, the platform cost barely registers.
- **Support that is mostly website chat with predictable volume** → SaaS First, after using the trial to measure real credit burn.
- **Operations teams where requests cross departments** → Front Professional. That is the specific problem it solves.
- **Support orgs of 20+ with managers running QA** → Front Enterprise, since the AI bundle beats buying add-ons piecemeal.
- **High-volume consumer support** → be skeptical of both. Front's per-seat plus metered model and SaaS First's flat $0.09 credit rate with no volume tier both punish scale.

## Bottom line

Price these two on their meters, not their headline rates. SaaS First is close to free as software and charges for chatbot conversations, which makes it excellent for a lean team with moderate inbound and poor value for anyone doing tens of thousands of conversations at list price. Front is expensive per seat and earns it only when conversation context crossing team boundaries is genuinely costing you money. If you cannot name the departments a ticket passes through, Front is probably too much tool.
