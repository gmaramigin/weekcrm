## TL;DR

- **BlueCamroo** ties CRM, projects, support tickets, time tracking, and invoicing to one client record, and bills by the User Roles each person carries — roughly $41 to $49 per user for a full role set, free for up to four CRM-only users.
- **Bitrix24** sells boxes with headcount written on them: $49 a month annually for 5 users, $99 for 50, $199 for 100, and inside a band extra people cost nothing.
- Both rate 3.9 in this directory, and both earn that partly through setup difficulty.
- Above roughly a dozen people, Bitrix24's economics are almost unanswerable. Below that, or with a sharply specialized team, BlueCamroo's model can win.

## Two ways to escape per-seat pricing

The interesting thing about putting these side by side is that neither vendor believes in the standard model, and they disagree about what should replace it.

BlueCamroo's answer is granularity. Assign a salesperson CRM roles, a delivery lead project roles, an operations manager several, and the bill is the sum. Nobody pays for capability they never open. A ten-person consultancy with four client-facing, three delivery, and three cross-functional people pays for exactly that distribution.

Bitrix24's answer is to stop counting people at all. Buy the band, fill it however you like. Twenty employees or forty-eight, Standard costs $99 a month either way.

The curves diverge sharply. At 50 users Bitrix24 works out under two dollars per person for a product that also includes telephony, HR tools, and an intranet. Fifty BlueCamroo users on full roles would run near $2,000 to $2,450. Any mid-sized company doing pure cost math lands on Bitrix24.

## Where the arithmetic flips

Two situations favor BlueCamroo.

The first is very small and CRM-only. Four users doing lead, contact, opportunity, and account management pay nothing on BlueCamroo — a real free tier, not a trial. Bitrix24's free plan caps at one or two users with 5 GB of storage, so a four-person sales team is already paying $49 a month on Basic for a platform whose density works against them at that size.

The second is a functionally split team where most people touch one area. If seven of your ten staff genuinely need only CRM, you buy seven cheap roles instead of ten full ones. Bitrix24 gives everyone everything whether or not they want it, which is fine when the platform is $2 a head and wasteful when it is not.

Where BlueCamroo's model breaks is a team where everyone needs everything. Then you are buying full role sets per person, plus the ongoing administrative job of auditing who carries which role — and roles drift upward over time unless someone actively prunes them.

## The tier boundary problem

Bitrix24's flat bands have a sharp edge. Standard covers exactly 50 users. Your 51st hire moves you to Professional and doubles the bill from $99 to $199, for a reason unrelated to software. The same cliff sits at 5 and at 100. If you are at 45 and growing, budget Professional now. If you are at 52, ask honestly whether two of those accounts are needed.

Monthly billing carries a steep penalty too — $69 against $49 on Basic, $144 against $99 on Standard, roughly 30% more for the privilege of not committing. Given the rollout effort, annual is effectively the default.

BlueCamroo has no such cliffs, but its quotes are configuration-dependent, so two companies of identical size can pay very differently. Storage and email marketing sit outside role licensing entirely, and for a business running volume campaigns the marketing add-on can outweigh the CRM roles.

## Depth and breadth

Bitrix24 is the broader product by a wide margin — CRM, kanban pipelines, projects, internal social network, HR, telephony, document storage, marketing automation, plus a self-hosted edition for data-residency requirements. The recurring complaint is not price but the interface: new users describe getting lost, and support is slow enough to be a real risk on complex issues.

BlueCamroo is narrower and more coherent. Leads, projects, tickets, time logs, and invoices connect through the client record, which is exactly what an agency or IT services firm needs. Its sales pipeline is functional but not deep, and its community is small — fewer third-party resources, fewer people who have solved your problem before.

Neither is a signup-and-go product. BlueCamroo's role system takes real configuration; Bitrix24's onboarding runs into weeks. Both punish teams with no internal owner, and a cheap platform nobody uses is the most expensive outcome on offer.

## Who should pick what

- **Companies of 15 to 100 consolidating several tools** → Bitrix24, on economics that nothing else matches.
- **Four-person teams needing CRM only** → BlueCamroo's free tier, at zero cost.
- **Agencies and consultancies where sales, delivery, and billing must share a client record** → BlueCamroo.
- **Teams with sharply specialized roles** → BlueCamroo, since you pay for the shape of the org rather than its size.
- **Businesses needing on-premise deployment** → Bitrix24, which is the only one of the two offering it.
- **Anyone who just wants a clean sales pipeline** → neither; both will overwhelm you.

## Bottom line

Bitrix24 is the better value and the worse experience. Its headcount bands make it nearly free per person at scale, and its feature density is genuinely hard to match, but plenty of teams never finish onboarding. BlueCamroo is the more thoughtful design for service businesses that bill their work, with a free tier that lets you prove the CRM before paying for anything else — just plan the role distribution before you request a quote, because that, not headcount, determines your price.
